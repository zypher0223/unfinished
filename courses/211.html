Volatility Smile & Skew 波动率微笑与偏斜

我的学习与错题新手路线

早期教科书里的经典定价模型假设：同一只股票、同一个到期日下，所有行权价的隐含波动率都应该相等。但打开真实的期权报价表（见本站「Implied Volatility, IV Rank & Volatility Crush 隐含波动率、IV Rank 与波动率压缩」一课）就会发现，不同行权价的 IV 高低不一。这是因为现实金融市场存在暴涨暴跌的“肥尾风险”，交易者对尾部保险的供需不平衡塑造了微笑与偏斜曲线。

English introduction

Textbook baseline pricing models originally assumed that every strike price sharing the same underlying and expiration date should trade at the exact same Implied Volatility. Yet inspecting any live option chain (see the "Implied Volatility, IV Rank & Volatility Crush" lesson on this site) reveals that IV varies noticeably across strikes. Because real-world markets experience fat-tailed jumps and crashes, supply-and-demand imbalances for tail protection bend the horizontal line into a smile or a skew.

提出者与来源

1987 年 10 月 19 日美股“黑色星期一”崩盘后，美股期权市场形成持久的下行波动率偏斜 — 在 1987 年 10 月之前，美股期权不同行权价的隐含波动率曲线相对平坦，接近 Black–Scholes 模型的常数波动率假设。1987 年 10 月 19 日标普 500 指数单日暴跌逾 20% 后，期权做市商与机构彻底重估了左尾崩盘风险，低行权价看跌期权从此长期维持显著的隐含波动率溢价，形成了延续至今的波动率偏斜结构。

Prior to October 1987, implied volatilities across U.S. equity option strikes were relatively flat, roughly matching the constant-volatility assumption of Black–Scholes. After the S&P 500 plunged over 20% on Black Monday (October 19, 1987), market makers and institutions permanently repriced left-tail crash risk, embedding a structural downside volatility skew across equity index options.

English written lesson

Think of it as catastrophe insurance pricing: in equity markets, institutional investors hold massive long stock portfolios and fear sudden market crashes, so they persistently bid up out-of-the-money downside Puts as crash insurance while selling upside Calls—making low-strike IV much more expensive than high-strike IV and tilting the curve into a lopsided smirk (skew).
