My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Auctions reveal the yield investors demand to absorb government borrowing.

Start with an everyday comparison

A borrower invites bids; a higher required yield means a higher borrowing cost and generally a lower bond price.

What does this number measure?

Yields use percent, tails use basis points and bid-to-cover is a ratio. Compare similar maturities and sizes.

Why can markets react?

Weak demand can lift Treasury yields, discount rates and crypto funding pressure; strong demand may also reflect fear rather than optimism.

Read the release step by step

  1. Check the official reference period, publication date, unit and seasonal adjustment.
  2. Read the named headline and its components; distinguish levels, changes and revised observations.
  3. Compare actual, independently sourced consensus and revised prior values; do not manufacture a consensus if none is available.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

A stronger-than-expected auction with orderly yield declines may support risk valuations.

When a fall is plausible

A material auction tail with poor absorption and persistent yield rises can hurt risk assets.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

Conflicting demand metrics or an expected result can leave markets mixed.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical: when-issued yield 4.20%, stop 4.23%, a 3bp tail. Comparing only with a prior 4.40% auction misses the changed pre-auction market.

History and origin

Treasury auctions evolved over time; Treasury bills were introduced through auctions in 1929. Modern uniform-price rules developed later.

Where and how to find the data

U.S. Treasury / TreasuryDirect · Scheduled auctions · Timing differs for bills and coupon securities; read each auction announcement.

U.S. Treasury / TreasuryDirect official data

  1. Navigate to Auctions → Auction Results rather than Announcements alone.
  2. Filter by security type and auction date, and open the result table or PDF.
  3. Read High Yield/Rate, Bid-to-Cover and allotments; compare with the same-maturity when-issued yield immediately before the auction.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. Bid-to-cover alone is insufficient; inspect the tail, allotments and subsequent yield response.
  2. Markets react to new information relative to expectations. Check yields, the dollar and price confirmation; the release cannot guarantee a trade.
