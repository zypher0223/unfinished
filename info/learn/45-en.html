Pending Home Sales Index

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Pending sales track existing-home contracts before closing and can lead completed sales.

Start with an everyday comparison

Reservations foreshadow diners, but cancellations prevent some from becoming sales.

What does this number measure?

These are index points, not unit counts or the percentage of houses sold; 100 refers to the 2001 benchmark.

Why can markets react?

Contracts indicate future housing-related demand and rate sensitivity; broad effects operate through growth and policy expectations.

Read the release step by step

  1. Check the official reference period, publication date, unit and seasonal adjustment.
  2. Read the named headline and its components; distinguish levels, changes and revised observations.
  3. Compare actual, independently sourced consensus and revised prior values; do not manufacture a consensus if none is available.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

A positive surprise with stable cancellation rates may support risk appetite.

When a fall is plausible

Persistent weakness alongside poorer jobs and credit can reduce growth expectations.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

Mixed regional data or closing bottlenecks can leave the signal unresolved.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical: an index of 80 rises to 84, a 5% monthly gain. Being below 100 does not imply house prices are 16% below the benchmark.

History and origin

The index uses 2001 average contract activity as 100; the methodology identifies that as the first analyzed year, not necessarily its public launch.

Where and how to find the data

National Association of Realtors NAR · Monthly · Usually 10:00 a.m. U.S. Eastern; confirm the NAR calendar.

National Association of Realtors NAR official data

  1. Open the latest news release and Monthly Index.
  2. Read the contract index, monthly percentage change and regional components.
  3. Open Methodology to confirm 2001=100 and the lag from signing to closing.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. A signed contract may fail to close because of financing, cancellations or other obstacles.
  2. Markets react to new information relative to expectations. Check yields, the dollar and price confirmation; the release cannot guarantee a trade.
