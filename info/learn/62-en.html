Net Marketable Treasury Borrowing

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Net issuance equals new borrowing minus maturing debt repaid; gross issuance can mostly refinance old debt.

Start with an everyday comparison

Borrowing $100 and repaying $80 adds $20, not $100.

What does this number measure?

Usually billions; distinguish estimates, revisions and settled actuals, plus privately held marketable borrowing from total debt.

Why can markets react?

Coupon supply can lift term premia; bills may draw money from RRP. Reserve and risk effects depend on maturities and purchasers.

Read the release step by step

  1. Check the official reference period, publication date, unit and seasonal adjustment.
  2. Read the named headline and its components; distinguish levels, changes and revised observations.
  3. Compare actual, independently sourced consensus and revised prior values; do not manufacture a consensus if none is available.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

Lower-than-expected financing needs with easing term pressure may support risk assets.

When a fall is plausible

A coupon-supply surprise with insufficient demand can lift yields and hurt valuations.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

Expected issuance or bills absorbed smoothly from RRP may have limited effects.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical: $1tn gross issuance less $800bn maturities equals $200bn net, $50bn below a $250bn forecast. Gross supply is not net drainage.

History and origin

Net borrowing is an accounting measure within Treasury’s evolving financing process; no single trader created it and no unverified first release date is asserted.

Where and how to find the data

U.S. Department of the Treasury · Quarterly plans and periodic actuals · Quarterly financing documents are issued in stages; planned borrowing differs from settled issuance.

U.S. Department of the Treasury official data

  1. Open Most Recent Documents under Quarterly Refunding.
  2. Choose Marketable Borrowing Estimates and the quarterly refunding statement.
  3. Find privately-held net marketable borrowing, assumed cash balances and the bill/coupon issuance mix.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. Issuance differs from settlement, and cash targets mean net borrowing differs from the deficit.
  2. Markets react to new information relative to expectations. Check yields, the dollar and price confirmation; the release cannot guarantee a trade.
