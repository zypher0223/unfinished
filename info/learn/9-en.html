Employment Cost Index (ECI)

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Measures changes in hourly employer compensation, including wages and benefits, while controlling for shifts in the mix of jobs.

Start with an everyday comparison

Think of the economy as a large store. If price tags rise too quickly, the central bank may keep the rent on borrowed money—interest—higher. The surprise relative to expectations matters as much as whether prices rose.

What does this number measure?

Units: seasonally adjusted quarterly %; unadjusted 12-month %. Distinguish a level from a change: a level is the current size; a change compares periods. Locate total compensation for civilian workers, then wages and salaries and benefits separately.

Why can markets react?

The number affects prices through expectations: inflation pressure changes the expected Fed path, which can change Treasury yields and borrowing costs. Investors then revalue future earnings in today’s money, called discounting. Higher safe yields also raise the opportunity cost of risky holdings. Crypto has different cash-flow characteristics but remains exposed to dollar financing, leverage and risk appetite. ECI’s job-mix controls help identify genuine compensation pressure; productivity must also be considered to assess cost per unit of output.

Read the release step by step

  1. Check the title, country and reference period, then identify preliminary, final or revised status. The release month is not necessarily the month being measured.
  2. Locate total compensation for civilian workers, then wages and salaries and benefits separately.
  3. Place comparable actual, consensus forecast and revised prior values side by side. Consensus comes from analyst surveys or financial calendars, not usually from the publisher’s statistical table.
  4. Use the hypothetical example to interpret the number, then check composition, several periods and the response in Treasury yields and market prices. A correlation is not proof of a single cause.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

Potentially supportive: a softer-than-expected comparable reading is confirmed by components, without evidence of collapsing activity. If yields fall, cheaper financing may support equity valuations and crypto risk-taking. A soft surprise, resilient activity and market confirmation are all assumptions.

When a fall is plausible

Potentially negative: unexpectedly hot details postpone expected easing and push yields higher, pressuring valuations or leverage. A very weak reading can also be negative if demand is collapsing and recession fears outweigh easier policy. Low does not mechanically mean bullish.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

A range or muted reaction is plausible if actual matches expectations or a soft headline conflicts with hot details or revisions. If the information was already priced in, publication may add little direction.

Work through a made-up example

All numbers below are invented for practice, not current readings. Quarterly compensation rises 0.8% versus 1.0% expected; both wages and benefits slow while hiring stays positive. This may ease service-sector cost pressure without implying collapsing jobs.

History and origin

BLS introduced ECI in 1975 and first published private-industry wage and salary estimates in June 1976. Benefits and total compensation were added in 1981; program inception and first publication are distinct.

Where and how to find the data

US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) · Quarterly · Usually released at 8:30 a.m. US Eastern Time; use the publisher’s calendar for the actual date.

US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) — official release

  1. Open the latest release and check the title and reference period.
  2. Locate total compensation for civilian workers, then wages and salaries and benefits separately.
  3. Record units, actual and revised values. Obtain a comparable forecast from a financial calendar and check its time zone.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. A 0.8% quarterly change is not a 0.8% annual change; productivity gains can offset higher compensation per hour.
  2. Do not treat related components of one release as independent confirmations, or extrapolate one observation indefinitely.
  3. US Eastern Time changes with daylight saving. Set calendars to your time zone and confirm the date; holidays or shutdowns can change schedules.
