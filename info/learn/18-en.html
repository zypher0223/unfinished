JOLTS Job Openings

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Positions open on the final business day of the month, available to start within 30 days and actively being recruited for; demand, not completed hiring.

Start with an everyday comparison

Imagine a restaurant. More customers can lead to hiring, longer shifts and higher wages; weaker demand may first cut hours and later jobs. Each statistic is one snapshot that must be combined with other labor measures.

What does this number measure?

Units: seasonally adjusted opening levels and opening rates. Distinguish a level from a change: a level is the current size; a change compares periods. Read total nonfarm openings in JOLTS Table 1, then compare hires and layoffs to distinguish reduced recruitment from active job cuts.

Why can markets react?

Jobs and pay support household spending and company revenue, while a tight labor market may sustain wage and service inflation and delay Fed easing. Earnings and discount rates can therefore pull equities in opposite directions. Crypto is affected indirectly through dollar financing, liquidity and risk appetite, not through a direct link from new jobs to token purchases. Numerous vacancies can make hiring harder and encourage wage bidding. Fewer openings without more layoffs can ease wage pressure rather than signal recession.

Read the release step by step

  1. Check the title, country and reference period, then identify preliminary, final or revised status. The release month is not necessarily the month being measured.
  2. Read total nonfarm openings in JOLTS Table 1, then compare hires and layoffs to distinguish reduced recruitment from active job cuts.
  3. Place comparable actual, consensus forecast and revised prior values side by side. Consensus comes from analyst surveys or financial calendars, not usually from the publisher’s statistical table.
  4. Use the hypothetical example to interpret the number, then check composition, several periods and the response in Treasury yields and market prices. A correlation is not proof of a single cause.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

Potentially supportive: with inflation contained, stronger labor conditions can ease recession fears. In an overheating economy, orderly cooling without widespread job loss can support easing expectations. Identify whether inflation or recession is the dominant concern first.

When a fall is plausible

Potentially negative: excessive strength plus wage inflation can lift yields and pressure valuations, while abrupt weakness can damage income, demand and earnings expectations. Both stocks and crypto may face risk reduction, though not necessarily equal or identical reactions.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

Choppy trading is plausible when payrolls, unemployment, participation or wages disagree, or revisions offset the latest surprise. A first-seconds move is not a settled interpretation of cooling versus deterioration.

Work through a made-up example

All numbers below are invented for practice, not current readings. Openings fall from 8.0 to 7.5 million while layoffs are stable and jobs still grow. This may be normalization; sharply falling hiring and employment would support a recessionary reading.

History and origin

BLS began developing JOLTS in 1998, collecting data in 2000, publicly releasing experimental estimates in 2002, and made the series official in 2004. A December 2000 data start is not the publication launch.

Where and how to find the data

US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) · Monthly · Usually near the start of a month at 10:00 a.m. Eastern, for an earlier reference month; verify BLS’s schedule.

US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) — official release

  1. Open the latest release and check the title and reference period.
  2. Read total nonfarm openings in JOLTS Table 1, then compare hires and layoffs to distinguish reduced recruitment from active job cuts.
  3. Record units, actual and revised values. Obtain a comparable forecast from a financial calendar and check its time zone.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. Openings are not payroll gains; vacancies do not guarantee that every advertised position will be filled.
  2. Do not treat related components of one release as independent confirmations, or extrapolate one observation indefinitely.
  3. US Eastern Time changes with daylight saving. Set calendars to your time zone and confirm the date; holidays or shutdowns can change schedules.
