University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment Index

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Michigan surveys households about finances, economic expectations and buying conditions. It differs from Conference Board confidence.

Start with an everyday comparison

Two groups of friends can answer differently; compare each survey with its own history.

What does this number measure?

Sentiment uses index points; inflation expectations in the same release use percent. Preliminary and final readings update the same month.

Why can markets react?

Spending intentions affect earnings expectations. Inflation expectations can affect the anticipated policy path, indirectly influencing stocks and crypto.

Read the release step by step

  1. Identify preliminary versus final.
  2. Separate sentiment components from inflation expectations.
  3. Compare consensus, revisions, survey dates and actual spending.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

Improving sentiment with anchored inflation expectations may support risk appetite.

When a fall is plausible

Weak sentiment paired with rising expected inflation can hurt both growth and valuation.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

Small revisions or opposing growth and rate effects can leave markets choppy.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical: sentiment beats a forecast of 70 at 74, but long-run expected inflation rises from 3.0% to 3.5%. A higher yield reaction can offset the confidence beat.

History and origin

The Surveys of Consumers began in 1946 under George Katona and evolved into the present measures. Individual subseries need not date to the original survey.

Where and how to find the data

University of Michigan · Monthly preliminary and final · Preliminary and final releases follow the official calendar, usually at 10:00 a.m. U.S. Eastern.

University of Michigan official data

  1. Open Reports and select the latest monthly report.
  2. Find Index of Consumer Sentiment, Current Economic Conditions and Consumer Expectations.
  3. Use Data / Survey Information to check preliminary versus final, fieldwork dates and publication dates.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. Index points and percentage points are different units.
  2. Attitudes are not purchasing power or actual expenditure.
