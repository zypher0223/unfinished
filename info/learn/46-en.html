What does this number measure?

Index levels measure values relative to a base. Separate annual and monthly changes; rolling three-month inputs create lag.

Why can markets react?

Home values affect wealth, collateral and banks, but are not the CPI rent measure. Crypto mainly shares the rate and risk-appetite channels.

Read the release step by step

  1. Check the official reference period, publication date, unit and seasonal adjustment.
  2. Read the named headline and its components; distinguish levels, changes and revised observations.
  3. Compare actual, independently sourced consensus and revised prior values; do not manufacture a consensus if none is available.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

Orderly stabilization with better employment can reduce collateral concerns.

When a fall is plausible

A sharp fall can hurt collateral and spending; overheating can instead increase rate pressure.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

Cooling annual growth alongside a monthly rebound can give mixed signals.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical: 300 a year ago and 315 now equals 5% annual growth; 314 last month implies about 0.32% monthly growth, not 5%.

History and origin

The method draws on Karl Case and Robert Shiller repeat-sales research. S&P/Case-Shiller indices launched in 2006, with back-calculated histories earlier.

Where and how to find the data

S&P Dow Jones Indices · Monthly · Usually the last Tuesday of the month at 9:00 a.m. U.S. Eastern; observations are lagged.

S&P Dow Jones Indices official data

  1. Select U.S. National or 20-City within the index family.
  2. Open the latest release and factsheet; note the reference month.
  3. Read monthly and annual changes separately and check the seasonal-adjustment label.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. Repeat-sales data still have selection and timing limits and are not contemporaneous rents.
  2. Markets react to new information relative to expectations. Check yields, the dollar and price confirmation; the release cannot guarantee a trade.
