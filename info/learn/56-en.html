Commercial Bank Deposits H.8

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Deposits are customers’ claims and banks’ liabilities, useful for monitoring funding stability.

Start with an everyday comparison

Customer balances fund an institution while creating an obligation to repay.

What does this number measure?

Read billions and seasonal adjustment. Distinguish total outflows from shifts between bank groups.

Why can markets react?

Outflows can raise bank funding costs and limit lending, but transfers to money funds do not destroy dollars. Broad risk effects depend on credit stress.

Read the release step by step

  1. Record adjusted weekly Deposits for All Commercial Banks and the observation date.
  2. Inspect large, small and foreign-related banks to distinguish redistribution from aggregate outflow.
  3. H.8 normally has no standard consensus field; check multiweek trends, taxes, reclassifications and funding stress.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

Stable returning deposits and lower funding costs can ease bank fears.

When a fall is plausible

Persistent broad outflows forcing expensive funding or asset sales can hurt risk appetite.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

Equal shifts from small to large banks may have limited aggregate impact.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical: small banks lose $50bn and large banks gain $45bn; aggregate deposits fall only $5bn. This is mostly redistribution.

History and origin

H.8 builds on longstanding weekly banking data whose collection traces to 1917; today’s deposit definitions have evolved.

Where and how to find the data

Federal Reserve Board · Weekly · H.8 normally Friday at 4:15 p.m. U.S. Eastern; holidays can shift publication.

Federal Reserve Board official data

  1. Open the latest H.8 release.
  2. Select All Commercial Banks and find Deposits, then large and small bank categories.
  3. Read adjusted weekly levels and footnotes for mergers, reclassifications and other breaks.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. Tax payments, quarter ends and reclassifications can distort weekly changes.
  2. Markets react to new information relative to expectations. Check yields, the dollar and price confirmation; the release cannot guarantee a trade.
