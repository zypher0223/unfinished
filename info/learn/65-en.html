Stablecoin Reserve and Attestation Reports

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Stablecoin Reserve and Attestation Reports are periodic disclosures published by fiat-backed stablecoin issuers (such as the issuers of USDC or USDT) accompanied by independent third-party accountant attestation reports. They show market participants what specific dollar-denominated assets back the circulating stablecoin liabilities as of a stated cutoff timestamp, and document the verification procedures performed by independent certified public accountants on management's assertions.

Start with an everyday comparison

Think of a stablecoin attestation report like a prepaid voucher company hiring an independent accountant to inspect its vaults on a specific date: the accountant checks both how many $1 vouchers are circulating outside (liabilities) and whether the vault holds at least as much cash and immediately cashable short-term Treasury bills (reserves), rather than illiquid long-term IOUs.

What does this number measure?

Both reserve assets and circulating token liabilities are typically reported in U.S. dollars; key safety metrics include the Reserve Coverage Ratio (fair value of eligible reserve assets divided by circulating token liabilities, where 100% or higher indicates full nominal backing) and the Excess Reserve buffer (total reserve assets minus circulating liabilities). Crucially, a monthly or quarterly 'attestation' (or examination under AICPA standards) verifies management's reserve assertion at a single point in time; it is not a full-scope annual financial statement audit of the entire corporate group and internal controls, nor does it ensure future solvency.

Why can markets react?

Fiat-backed stablecoins serve as the crypto market's primary quote currency, settlement rail, and derivatives collateral: transparent reserves concentrated in highly liquid, short-duration assets (such as short-term U.S. Treasury Bills, overnight reverse repos, and regulated cash deposits) anchor confidence in the $1.00 peg. Conversely, reserve shortfalls, opaque corporate loans, duration mismatches, or custodian bank failures can trigger a secondary-market depeg, redemption runs, and forced deleveraging across crypto markets (though routine clean attestations rarely move broad U.S. equities).

Read the release step by step

  1. Download the latest official reserve breakdown and independent accountant's attestation PDF from the issuer's transparency page, verifying the exact as-of cutoff date/time and the publication date (which often lags by several weeks).
  2. Compare total circulating token liabilities against total reserve assets to verify ≥100% coverage and calculate any excess reserve cushion, then decompose the reserve portfolio into cash deposits, short-term U.S. Treasury Bills, overnight Treasury reverse repos, and any higher-risk or less-liquid loans, commercial paper, or other assets.
  3. Read the independent accountant's opinion (checking for an unqualified opinion), the attestation standard applied, custodian disclosures, and footnotes regarding encumbered assets or settlement timing differences.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

Confirmation of ≥100% reserve coverage with a healthy equity cushion and a high share of Tier-1 liquid assets (short-term T-Bills, overnight repos, and regulated cash) under an unqualified attestation reinforces confidence in crypto market settlement rails.

When a fall is plausible

Disclosures showing rising allocations to illiquid loans, custodian banking disruptions, delayed reporting, or qualified accountant opinions can spark depeg fears, redemption pressure, and broad crypto market volatility.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

Routine on-schedule monthly attestations that confirm expected ≥100% backing and unchanged high-liquidity portfolios are backward-looking compliance checks and typically leave crypto prices steady.

Work through a made-up example

Suppose a stablecoin issuer's month-end attestation shows $100 billion in circulating token liabilities and $103 billion in total reserve assets, yielding a $103B - $100B = $3 billion excess reserve cushion and a 103.0% headline coverage ratio ($103B / $100B = 103.0%). However, if only $80 billion of those reserves sit in short-term T-Bills and cash (an 80.0% immediate liquid coverage ratio, $80B / $100B = 80.0%) while $23 billion sits in less-liquid secured loans, one cannot assume zero liquidity risk from the 103.0% headline alone. History and origin

USDC launched in 2018 with monthly reserve attestations; disclosures expanded in 2022. Other stablecoins follow different reporting histories. In the United States, the GENIUS Act was signed on 18 July 2025; it requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers to publish their reserve composition monthly and have it examined by a registered public accounting firm. The OCC announced its final implementing rule on 21 October 2026, and the Act takes effect no later than 18 January 2027 (checked 13 November 2026). Until then, issuer disclosure practices still differ.

Where and how to find the data

Stablecoin issuers and independent accountants; Circle USDC example official data

  1. Under Monthly assurance and transparency select the report month.
  2. Download the signed independent accountant report rather than relying only on the summary page.
  3. Check observation dates, circulating liabilities, reserve value, composition, examination scope and the conclusion.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. Never equate a point-in-time reserve attestation with a full-scope annual financial statement audit, and never look only at the ≥100% headline ratio without inspecting the underlying liquidity, maturity profile, and custodian concentration of the reserve assets.
  2. Markets trade marginal new information relative to expectations. Because attestation reports arrive with a multi-week lag, monitor real-time on-chain supply changes, DEX stablecoin pool balances (such as Curve), and secondary market peg prices alongside the reports.
