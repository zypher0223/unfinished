Secured Overnight Financing Rate SOFR

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

SOFR summarizes overnight cash borrowing secured by Treasuries and serves as a funding benchmark.

Start with an everyday comparison

It resembles an observed secured overnight borrowing price rather than a survey quote.

What does this number measure?

The rate is annualized, not a one-night 5% charge. Daily SOFR, compounded averages and the SOFR index differ. Daily SOFR is a volume-weighted median, not a simple arithmetic mean. Daily SOFR is a volume-weighted median, not a simple arithmetic mean.

Why can markets react?

SOFR prices floating borrowing and derivatives; unusual funding spikes can force deleveraging, while orderly policy-linked moves reflect the rate path.

Read the release step by step

  1. Record Rate, transaction date, Volume and Percentiles; publication follows the transaction day.
  2. Compare administered policy rates and prior SOFR to separate policy shifts from extra funding stress.
  3. There is no official consensus column; inspect quarter ends, taxes, settlements, volume and revisions.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

Normalization of an abnormal funding spread can ease pressure on risk assets.

When a fall is plausible

A persistent non-policy spike with funding stress can trigger deleveraging.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

Temporary quarter-end moves or anticipated policy changes may have little broad effect.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical: policy-related rates are near 4% but SOFR prints 4.8%. That is not an announced 80bp hike; inspect settlement, taxes and persistence.

History and origin

The New York Fed began publishing SOFR in April 2018 using Treasury repo transactions; it is not simply a renamed LIBOR or a policy target.

Where and how to find the data

Federal Reserve Bank of New York · Every U.S. business day · Normally 8:00 a.m. U.S. Eastern for previous-business-day transactions; eligible revisions may follow.

Federal Reserve Bank of New York official data

  1. Open the official SOFR page.
  2. Read Rate, transaction date, Volume and Percentiles.
  3. Compare the daily rate with policy-administered rates and previous business days; read FAQs for compounded averages.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. SOFR is secured, unlike federal funds; one spike does not establish recession.
  2. Markets react to new information relative to expectations. Check yields, the dollar and price confirmation; the release cannot guarantee a trade.
