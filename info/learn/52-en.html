Reserve Balances of Depository Institutions

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

These are balances banks hold at Federal Reserve Banks for settlement, not household deposits or all vault cash.

Start with an everyday comparison

They resemble merchants’ settlement balances, a different layer from customers’ wallets.

What does this number measure?

Units are millions or billions; aggregate abundance does not guarantee equal distribution or a fixed flow into risk assets.

Why can markets react?

Scarcity or uneven distribution can strain funding and leverage, but lending also depends on capital, demand and risk limits.

Read the release step by step

  1. Find Reserve balances and keep weekly averages separate from Wednesday levels.
  2. Inspect TGA, RRP and securities movements to identify balance-sheet shifts.
  3. Without a reliable consensus, compare the previous week, tax and settlement dates, and SOFR versus administered rates.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

Improved reserves with easing funding spreads can reduce forced-selling pressure.

When a fall is plausible

Falling reserves alongside abnormal funding spikes can hurt risk appetite.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

A decline within an ample range or temporary tax-date swing may have little market effect.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical: reserves fall from $3tn to $2.9tn but funding spreads stay stable. The decline alone does not establish scarcity.

History and origin

Reserves arise from the central bank settlement system. Interest on reserves began in 2008 and the ample-reserves framework changed implementation.

Where and how to find the data

Federal Reserve Board · Weekly and monthly · H.4.1 usually Thursday at 4:30 p.m. U.S. Eastern; additional monthly data are in H.6.

Federal Reserve Board official data

  1. Open H.4.1 Table 1.
  2. Find Reserve balances with Federal Reserve Banks.
  3. Compare Wednesday levels with weekly averages and inspect TGA and reverse-repo movements.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. Do not treat household deposits and central bank reserves as the same pool.
  2. Markets react to new information relative to expectations. Check yields, the dollar and price confirmation; the release cannot guarantee a trade.
