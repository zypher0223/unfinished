Commercial Bank Loans H.8

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

It measures outstanding bank loans, not simply the week’s newly approved originations.

Start with an everyday comparison

An outstanding loan balance is a water level; lending adds water while repayment, charge-offs and sales remove it.

What does this number measure?

Balances are generally billions. Separate business, real-estate and consumer credit and inspect the growth-rate convention.

Why can markets react?

Loans finance spending and investment, but emergency credit-line draws can inflate balances; contraction may reflect demand or supply.

Read the release step by step

  1. Read Loans and Leases and confirm adjusted status and billion-dollar units.
  2. Separate business, property and consumer credit and inspect loan sales or reclassification notes.
  3. Usually no standardized consensus exists; compare several weeks, SLOOS and demand to distinguish weak supply from weak demand.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

Healthy business lending with contained defaults can support growth and risk appetite.

When a fall is plausible

Shrinking loans with tighter standards and weaker jobs can signal a credit crunch.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

Sales or accounting shifts can explain movements without broad demand implications.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical: $10bn new lending, $8bn repayment and $3bn sales produce a $1bn decline in balances despite new loans.

History and origin

The measure comes from H.8 and its longstanding weekly reporting system; loan categories and accounting coverage have evolved.

Where and how to find the data

Federal Reserve Board · Weekly · H.8 normally Friday at 4:15 p.m. U.S. Eastern; verify the calendar.

Federal Reserve Board official data

  1. Open H.8 and choose All Commercial Banks.
  2. Find Loans and Leases, Commercial and Industrial, Real Estate and Consumer components.
  3. Compare outstanding balances with the correct growth convention and check loan-sale or reclassification notes.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. Rising balances may reflect credit-line draws rather than fresh investment.
  2. Markets react to new information relative to expectations. Check yields, the dollar and price confirmation; the release cannot guarantee a trade.
