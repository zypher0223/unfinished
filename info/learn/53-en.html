Overnight Reverse Repurchase Facility ON RRP

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

The Overnight Reverse Repurchase Facility (ON RRP), published every U.S. business day around 1:15 p.m. Eastern by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, measures the total dollar amount of excess cash that eligible counterparties—primarily money market funds (MMFs), Government-Sponsored Enterprises (GSEs), and banks—park overnight at the Federal Reserve in exchange for Treasury collateral and an administered interest rate. It tracks idle institutional cash sitting in the Fed's overnight parking lot rather than central-bank money printing. Beginners often confuse Fed Reverse Repo (which absorbs cash) with Repo (which injects cash), or assume cash leaving ON RRP flows directly into stocks or Bitcoin.

Start with an everyday comparison

Think of ON RRP as an official overnight parking garage run by the Fed that pays a assured floor rate to money market funds: when outside Treasury bill yields pay less than the Fed's garage, trillions of dollars park inside; when the Treasury issues more T-bills at slightly higher yields than the garage rate, money funds drive their cash out of the garage to buy T-bills—moving from one safe money-market instrument to another, not into a casino.

What does this number measure?

Reported daily in billions of dollars, showing Total Accepted volume (the day's ON RRP balance), the number of participating Counterparties, and the administered ON RRP Award Rate (which acts as the floor for U.S. overnight money-market rates). Be careful to distinguish the domestic Overnight Reverse Repo facility (ON RRP) from the separate Foreign Official and International Accounts reverse-repo pool shown on theH.4.1 release.

Why can markets react?

Transmission works through balance-sheet plumbing: when the Treasury issues short-term T-bills at yields slightly above the ON RRP rate → money market funds withdraw cash from ON RRP to buy those T-bills → allowing the Treasury to raise cash (or the Fed to run QT) by draining the ON RRP buffer rather than draining commercial Bank Reserves (Lesson 52) → keeping bank reserves ample and repo rates stable → indirectly supporting equity valuations and crypto liquidity conditions. Once the ON RRP buffer is largely depleted near zero, further QT or Treasury cash building must come directly out of Bank Reserves.

Read the release step by step

  1. Verify the domestic ON RRP operation: open the New York Fed's Reverse Repo Operations page after 1:15 p.m. Eastern and check the Overnight results (not the Foreign Official pool).
  2. Read Accepted Amount, Counterparties, and Rate: note the daily dollar balance (in billions), number of participants, and the administered award rate.
  3. Compare T-bill yields and quarter-end spikes: compare 1- to 3-month T-bill yields and SOFR against the ON RRP rate, and expect sharp temporary spikes on the final days of March, June, September, and December when banks trim balance sheets for regulatory reporting.
  4. Cross-check Reserves and TGA in H.4.1: pair weekly changes in ON RRP with Bank Reserves and the TGA to verify where the cash actually went.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

If cash drains smoothly out of ON RRP to absorb new T-bill supply during QT—keeping Bank Reserves stable and overnight repo spreads calm—it shows the liquidity buffer is shielding the banking system and can support risk appetite across equities and crypto.

When a fall is plausible

If the ON RRP buffer is already drained near zero so that further QT or TGA increases pull directly from Bank Reserves—or if acute market stress drives a flight back into ON RRP while private repo funding tightens—liquidity concerns can weigh on stocks and crypto.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

If ON RRP spikes by $150B–$300B on the last business day of a quarter (window dressing) and drops right back down on the first day of the new quarter, it is a routine calendar artifact that broad markets ignore.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical numbers for illustration only: suppose the Fed's administered ON RRP rate is 5.30%. In Phase 1, 1-month Treasury bills yield 5.22% (8 bps below ON RRP), so money market funds park $2,000B in ON RRP. In Phase 2, heavy T-bill issuance pushes 1-month T-bill yields up to 5.38% (5.38% - 5.30% = +8 bps above ON RRP). To earn the extra 8 bps, money funds withdraw $500B from ON RRP (reducing ON RRP from $2,000B to $1,500B) to buy $500B of new T-bills, lifting the Treasury's TGA by +$500B while leaving commercial Bank Reserves unchanged at $0 net change. The $500B rotated from ON RRP into T-bills, not into equities or crypto.

History and origin

Fixed-rate overnight reverse-repo testing began September 23, 2013; repo transactions themselves are much older.

Where and how to find the data

Federal Reserve Bank of New York · Every U.S. business day · Operation usually 12:45–1:15 p.m. U.S. Eastern, with results afterward; verify current parameters.

Federal Reserve Bank of New York official data

  1. Open Reverse Repo Operations and select results.
  2. Choose Overnight Reverse Repo, not the foreign-official reverse-repo pool.
  3. Record accepted amount, counterparty count, rate and date; compare bill yields and reserve conditions.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. Mixing up central bank terminology: at the U.S. Federal Reserve, a Reverse Repo (ON RRP) absorbs cash from counterparties (the opposite of the People's Bank of China's terminology, where a 'reverse repo' injects cash).
  2. Assuming that when $500B leaves ON RRP, money market funds used it to buy stocks or Bitcoin—under SEC Rule 2a-7, government money market funds are legally restricted to cash, Treasuries, and government repos.
  3. Misinterpreting routine quarter-end (March 31, June 30, Sept 30, Dec 31) balance-sheet window-dressing spikes in ON RRP as a sudden macro shock.
