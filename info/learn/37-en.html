Conference Board Consumer Confidence Index

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

A household survey of attitudes toward jobs, income and business conditions. It is not a receipt-based spending count.

Start with an everyday comparison

Asking neighbors whether they plan to dine out is useful to a restaurant, but intentions are not paid orders.

What does this number measure?

Index points are not percentages of optimistic households. The headline combines current conditions and expectations.

Why can markets react?

Better confidence can improve revenue expectations; overheating can raise the expected rate path. Crypto mainly receives the risk-appetite and dollar-rate effects.

Read the release step by step

  1. Identify the Conference Board release, not Michigan.
  2. Read headline versus consensus and inspect employment and expectations.
  3. Check revisions and survey dates; verify sentiment with retail spending.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

A positive surprise without renewed inflation pressure can support a soft landing.

When a fall is plausible

An overheating interpretation or a jobs-driven confidence collapse can both hurt markets through different channels.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

Mixed components or an anticipated release can produce little net movement.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical: forecast 100, actual 105, revised prior 102. If current conditions improve but expectations fall, the headline does not establish stronger future spending.

History and origin

The survey began in 1967; the index uses 1985 as its reference value of 100. This is survey history, not the invention of a trading rule.

Where and how to find the data

The Conference Board · Monthly · Usually the last Tuesday of the month at 10:00 a.m. U.S. Eastern; confirm the calendar.

The Conference Board official data

  1. Open the latest Consumer Confidence news release.
  2. Locate Consumer Confidence Index, Present Situation Index and Expectations Index separately.
  3. Open Technical Notes to check the reference base, survey dates and prior-month revision.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. 105 is not 105% optimism.
  2. Survey responses do not guarantee purchases or a recession.
