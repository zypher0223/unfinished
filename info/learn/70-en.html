Commercial crude stocks generally exclude the Strategic Petroleum Reserve. Draws and builds can arise from demand, imports or refinery activity.

Start with an everyday comparison

An emptying warehouse can reflect stronger sales or a late delivery.

What does this number measure?

Tables often use thousand barrels while headlines use million barrels; negative changes are draws. Separate crude and products.

Why can markets react?

Stocks influence oil prices, energy earnings, consumer costs and inflation. Higher oil can help producers while hurting broad equities and crypto through rates.

Read the release step by step

  1. Check the official reference period, publication date, unit and seasonal adjustment.
  2. Read the named headline and its components; distinguish levels, changes and revised observations.
  3. Compare actual, independently sourced consensus and revised prior values; do not manufacture a consensus if none is available.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

Supply-led builds that ease inflation without weak demand can help broad risk assets, though producers may lag.

When a fall is plausible

A supply-shock draw or recession-driven build can both hurt broad markets through different channels.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

Crude draws with product builds or temporary trade shifts can give mixed signals.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical: a 5m-barrel crude draw versus 2m expected comes with a 4m gasoline build and higher refinery runs. Crude may have moved into products rather than final demand.

History and origin

EIA was established in 1977; the weekly reporting system and historical series evolved separately, so agency formation is not every component’s first release.

Where and how to find the data

U.S. Energy Information Administration EIA · Weekly · Usually Wednesday at 10:30 a.m. U.S. Eastern; holidays may delay it.

U.S. Energy Information Administration EIA official data

  1. Open Weekly Petroleum Status Report.
  2. Find U.S. commercial crude oil inventories excluding SPR and the weekly change.
  3. Read gasoline and distillate stocks, refinery runs, trade flows and adjustments before judging demand.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. API is a separate estimate; SPR releases and balancing adjustments require context.
  2. Markets react to new information relative to expectations. Check yields, the dollar and price confirmation; the release cannot guarantee a trade.
