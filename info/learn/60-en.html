Discount Window Borrowing Primary Credit

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Eligible banks borrow against collateral from the Fed; primary-credit usage is a liquidity backstop, not retail lending.

Start with an everyday comparison

It is an emergency funding counter, but borrowing alone does not prove insolvency.

What does this number measure?

Tables usually use millions and show outstanding usage, not capacity; separate primary, secondary and other facilities.

Why can markets react?

Borrowing can prevent fire sales but surging usage may reveal funding stress; markets weigh backstop benefits against underlying trouble.

Read the release step by step

  1. Under Loans choose Primary credit and consistently use Wednesday levels or weekly averages.
  2. Keep primary, secondary, seasonal and other emergency facilities distinct.
  3. Without standardized consensus, compare prior usage, deposits and funding spreads before assessing the backstop.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

A successful backstop with stabilizing deposits and spreads can help risk sentiment recover.

When a fall is plausible

Surging usage with runs or losses may cause selling despite balance-sheet expansion.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

Seasonal or technical usage by a few institutions may have little broad effect.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical: primary credit jumps from $5bn to $50bn amid deposit losses. The extra $45bn is not automatically bullish liquidity.

History and origin

Discount-window lending dates to the Fed’s early operations in 1914; the modern primary-credit program began in 2003.

Where and how to find the data

Federal Reserve Board · Weekly · H.4.1 normally Thursday at 4:30 p.m. U.S. Eastern.

Federal Reserve Board official data

  1. Open H.4.1 Table 1.
  2. Under Loans find Primary credit; keep Secondary credit and Seasonal credit separate.
  3. Compare Wednesday levels and weekly averages, then check bank deposits and funding spreads.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. Liquidity and solvency differ; borrowing is not a direct capital-loss measure.
  2. Markets react to new information relative to expectations. Check yields, the dollar and price confirmation; the release cannot guarantee a trade.
