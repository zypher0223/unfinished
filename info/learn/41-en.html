Housing Starts

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Starts count privately owned housing units where construction has begun, not homes completed.

Start with an everyday comparison

A permit is permission; a start is beginning the renovation; completion is opening the doors.

What does this number measure?

Headlines usually use a seasonally adjusted annual rate; 1.2 million is an annualized pace, not one month’s construction. Multifamily units can be volatile.

Why can markets react?

Construction supports jobs and materials demand; overheating can keep rates high. Crypto responds mainly through growth, rates and risk appetite.

Read the release step by step

  1. Check the official reference period, publication date, unit and seasonal adjustment.
  2. Read the named headline and its components; distinguish levels, changes and revised observations.
  3. Compare actual, independently sourced consensus and revised prior values; do not manufacture a consensus if none is available.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

A moderate upside surprise without inflation pressure may support a soft landing and risk assets.

When a fall is plausible

A collapse may signal weak demand, while overheating may raise yields; either can hurt through different channels.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

A multifamily-led jump, weather rebound or mixed components may leave markets choppy.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical: forecast 1.30m, actual 1.40m, but single-family falls from 1.00m to 0.95m. A multifamily jump does not establish broad household demand.

History and origin

Census took responsibility for construction statistics in 1959; the modern comparable series starts then, with predecessor estimates earlier.

Where and how to find the data

U.S. Census Bureau / HUD · Monthly · Usually 8:30 a.m. U.S. Eastern on the Census calendar; delays and holidays can change dates.

U.S. Census Bureau / HUD official data

  1. Open Latest New Residential Construction Report.
  2. Find Housing Starts, then single-family and multifamily rows.
  3. Compare starts with permits and completions, checking annualized units and revised months.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. Starts are neither transactions nor prices; weather, sampling uncertainty and revisions matter.
  2. Markets react to new information relative to expectations. Check yields, the dollar and price confirmation; the release cannot guarantee a trade.
