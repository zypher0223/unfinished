A common analytical grouping excludes autos, gasoline, building materials and food services to inform estimates of underlying goods consumption.

Start with an everyday comparison

Use a household budget: income is money received, disposable income is after personal taxes, and consumption is money spent. More dollars do not necessarily buy more goods when prices rise.

What does this number measure?

Units: seasonally adjusted nominal monthly %; verify exclusions. Distinguish a level from a change: a level is the current size; a change compares periods. Use the official advance table to verify the total and four excluded categories; check that a calendar’s control-group definition matches.

Why can markets react?

Household spending becomes business revenue, and sustainable real income supports future demand, affecting equity earnings expectations. Excess demand can also delay disinflation and rate cuts. Crypto is mainly exposed through dollar rates, risk sentiment and investable funds; rising income does not automatically flow into stocks or tokens. The control group removes several volatile categories to inform GDP goods-consumption inputs; further price and conceptual adjustments are still needed.

Read the release step by step

  1. Check the title, country and reference period, then identify preliminary, final or revised status. The release month is not necessarily the month being measured.
  2. Use the official advance table to verify the total and four excluded categories; check that a calendar’s control-group definition matches.
  3. Place comparable actual, consensus forecast and revised prior values side by side. Consensus comes from analyst surveys or financial calendars, not usually from the publisher’s statistical table.
  4. Use the hypothetical example to interpret the number, then check composition, several periods and the response in Treasury yields and market prices. A correlation is not proof of a single cause.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

Potentially supportive: real demand or after-tax purchasing power is more resilient than expected, financed sustainably and without worsening inflation. An interpretation of continued growth without tighter policy can support earnings and crypto sentiment.

When a fall is plausible

Potentially negative: stronger dollars merely reflect inflation while purchasing power shrinks, or demand drops enough to threaten profits. Overheated consumption can also lift yields and pressure valuations. Separate income, quantities and prices first.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

A mixed response is plausible if spending is strong but income weak and households rely on lower saving or borrowing, or goods and services diverge. In-line actuals and offsetting revisions can produce only temporary volatility.

History and origin

Census has conducted the Monthly Retail Trade Survey since 1951. Advance estimates and modern groupings evolved later; that date does not establish the launch of today’s exact advance aggregate or analytical control group.

Where and how to find the data

US Census Bureau · Monthly · Advance sales are usually released mid-month at 8:30 a.m. Eastern; verify the Census economic indicator calendar.

US Census Bureau — official release

  1. Open the latest release and check the title and reference period.
  2. Use the official advance table to verify the total and four excluded categories; check that a calendar’s control-group definition matches.
  3. Record units, actual and revised values. Obtain a comparable forecast from a financial calendar and check its time zone.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. The control group is neither real consumption nor all services; some calendars use core retail to mean ex-autos only.
  2. Do not treat related components of one release as independent confirmations, or extrapolate one observation indefinitely.
  3. US Eastern Time changes with daylight saving. Set calendars to your time zone and confirm the date; holidays or shutdowns can change schedules.
