Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey SLOOS

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

It asks whether banks tightened standards and whether borrowers’ demand changed; supply and demand are separate.

Start with an everyday comparison

Ask a shop whether it tightened credit terms and separately whether more customers asked for credit.

What does this number measure?

Net tightening is the percentage reporting tightening minus easing, not a loan interest rate or a percentage drop in volume.

Why can markets react?

Tighter access can restrain investment and spending; lower demand can signal slowing growth. Crypto is affected indirectly through credit and risk appetite.

Read the release step by step

  1. Read whether each question asks about tightening or stronger demand before interpreting its sign.
  2. Separate loan categories and compare standards with demand.
  3. Where no reliable consensus exists, compare quarters; survey net percentages are not actual loan volumes.

Three possible market reactions

These are conditional explanations, not fixed trading rules. The same release can be interpreted differently when inflation, recession or funding stress dominates.

When a rise is plausible

Less tightening with stabilizing demand can support a soft landing.

When a fall is plausible

Rapid tightening combined with collapsing demand can increase recession fears.

When a mixed or sideways response is plausible

Improving standards but weak demand can produce conflicting signals.

Work through a made-up example

Hypothetical: 40% tighten and 10% ease, giving net tightening of 30 points. Falling to 20 means less widespread tightening, not net easing.

History and origin

The survey began in 1964 with 120 banks and quarterly reporting; panels and questions evolved, with four surveys a year typical since 1992.

Where and how to find the data

Federal Reserve Board · Usually quarterly · Normally around the January/February, April/May, July/August and October/November policy cycles.

Federal Reserve Board official data

  1. Choose the latest dated SLOOS release.
  2. Read Standards and Demand separately for business, property and household lending.
  3. Check the definition and sign of net percentage; net tightening is not an interest rate.

Check the current official release calendar. Time zones, daylight saving, holidays and revisions can change what you see; never assume a displayed local time is New York time.

Common mistakes

  1. Signs depend on the question; qualitative changes are not actual loan prices.
  2. Markets react to new information relative to expectations. Check yields, the dollar and price confirmation; the release cannot guarantee a trade.
