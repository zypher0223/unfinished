Multi-timeframe Analysis

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

The same stretch of price can be a pullback in an uptrend on the daily chart and a downtrend on the 15-minute chart. Multi-timeframe analysis reads the big picture first and the details second, so you do not misjudge direction by staring at one timeframe.

DefinitionMulti-timeframe analysis uses a higher timeframe to set the context (trend direction and key levels) and a lower timeframe to watch how price behaves at those levels. Common ratios are about 4–6×, such as 4-hour with 1-hour, or daily with 4-hour. When the two conflict, the higher timeframe’s context is the premise and the lower timeframe only informs timing.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like checking the map to choose the city, then the street plan to choose the turning.

A made-up example

Illustration only: on the 4-hour chart price is in an uptrend and returns to former resistance near 100; on the 1-hour chart a bar between 100 and 100.5 closes back above the prior bar’s high, giving a lower-timeframe reference at that level.

Who developed or popularized it?

No single inventor verified; Alexander Elder’s Triple Screen is a well-known systematic application

Watching several timeframes goes back to Dow Theory’s primary and secondary trends; Elder’s Triple Screen in the 1980s turned it into rules (see lesson 167).

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Pick a pair of timeframes about 4–6× apart, such as 4-hour and 1-hour.
  2. Mark the trend direction and key levels on the higher timeframe.
  3. Switch to the lower timeframe and watch price only near those levels.
  4. If a higher-timeframe key level is broken on a close, lower-timeframe signals lose their context; reassess.

The mistake to avoid

The most common error is flipping between timeframes until one agrees with what you already believe; fix the timeframe pair in advance.
