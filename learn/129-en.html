Inside & Outside Bars

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

DefinitionAn inside bar has a high below the previous high and a low above the previous low; an outside bar has a high above the previous high and a low below the previous low. This lesson uses the strict version, so touching an extreme does not count.

An inside bar fits entirely inside the previous bar, like a small stick inside a box, while an outside bar sticks out at both ends. Only highs and lows are compared, not candle colour or body length.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Check whether a small stick fits entirely inside the previous box or sticks out at both ends.

A made-up example

Illustration only: a mother bar has a high of 102.80 and a low of 100.80; the next bar has a high of 102.30 and a low of 101.30, inside the mother bar, so it is an inside bar; the following bar has a high of 103.10 and a low of 100.50, beyond both ends, so it is an outside bar.

Who developed or popularized it?

No single inventor verified; Al Brooks is a key contemporary educator

Inside and outside bars are combinations defined by comparing neighbouring highs and lows, taught systematically in modern price-action courses by educators such as Al Brooks, who is not presented here as their sole inventor.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Open the data window and compare neighbouring highs and lows one by one, rather than estimating by eye.
  2. Classify the bar first: inside, outside, or an ordinary directional bar that breaks only one side.
  3. Note the signal bar high and low as the two observation edges.
  4. Wait for a later close outside one edge before updating the reading, and treat a return inside the range as invalidation.

The mistake to avoid

A common confusion is to treat an inside bar as a harami. A harami compares bodies, while an inside bar compares whole highs and lows, shadows included.
