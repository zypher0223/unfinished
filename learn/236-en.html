MEV & Sandwich Attacks

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

On Ethereum, a transaction you send first sits in the public pool of pending transactions (the mempool), visible to everyone, before it is included in a block. That lets others act on your transaction ahead of it. Not all MEV harms users: arbitrage pulls prices on different venues together, and liquidations keep lending protocols solvent. Sandwich attacks, however, take value directly from traders. You control two main things: slippage tolerance (the gap you accept between the quoted price and the worst fill) and how the transaction is sent. Some services offer private transaction routes, such as an RPC endpoint (the entry point a wallet uses to reach a node) like Flashbots Protect, which keeps your transaction out of the public mempool and reduces the chance of being frontrun or sandwiched; it lowers the risk rather than removing it, and you have to trust the provider.

DefinitionMEV (maximal extractable value) is the extra value, beyond the block reward and fees, that can be gained during block production by choosing which transactions to include, exclude or reorder. Common forms are DEX arbitrage, liquidations and frontrunning. The one that hits ordinary users most directly is the sandwich attack: a bot sees your large swap waiting in the public pool of pending transactions, buys just before you and sells just after, so you fill at a worse price and the bot keeps the difference.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like queuing for limited sneakers: a scalper hears you want 20 pairs, buys the first few ahead of you and resells them to you at a mark-up; you get your shoes, but each pair costs more.

A made-up example

Illustration only (simplified, ignoring fees and your own price impact): you swap 10,000 USDC for a token quoted at 1.00, expecting 10,000 ÷ 1.00 = 10,000 tokens. Your slippage tolerance is 3%, so the worst acceptable price is 1.03. A bot buys first and pushes the price to 1.025, still within your tolerance, so you fill at 1.025 and get 10,000 ÷ 1.025 ≈ 9,756 tokens, about 10,000 − 9,756 = 244 fewer; the bot then sells and keeps the difference. With 0.5% tolerance (worst price 1.005), the push to 1.025 makes your transaction fail instead of filling badly, leaving the bot nothing to take.

Who developed or popularized it?

No single inventor: MEV arises from public mempools and the ability to order transactions in a block

ethereum.org defines MEV as the value beyond the block reward and fees obtainable during block production by including, excluding or reordering transactions. Flashbots is an organisation researching and mitigating MEV and offers tools such as Flashbots Protect; the Ethereum community is also discussing changes such as proposer-builder separation (PBS).

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Before a large swap, read the estimated price impact and the “minimum received”; skip pools where the impact is large.
  2. Set slippage tolerance to what you need, and do not raise it carelessly just because a swap failed.
  3. Consider protections such as private transaction routes (Flashbots Protect is one example, not a recommendation), knowing they depend on the provider.
  4. Split large amounts into several swaps or use an aggregator or limit orders to reduce the impact of any single trade.

The mistake to avoid

Raising slippage tolerance to 10% or more because swaps keep failing. That tells bots you will accept almost any price, which is exactly the room a sandwich attack needs.
