Head & Shoulders

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

DefinitionHead and shoulders top: of three swing highs, the middle “head” is higher than the left and right “shoulders”; the two lows between them define the neckline. The pattern completes only with a close below the neckline. A common reference distance is the head-to-neckline height projected down from the break; it is a reference, not a target. The inverse pattern is the bottom.

A head and shoulders top looks like a person’s outline: left shoulder, a higher head, right shoulder. It describes buying power fading: the right shoulder cannot reach the head. Only a close below the neckline completes the pattern; before that it is just a possibility.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like a hike where your third climb is clearly lower than the second: your energy is fading.

A made-up example

Illustration only: left shoulder 104, head 105.5, right shoulder 104.2, and the lows between the shoulders near 102.6 form the neckline. Only a close below 102.6 completes it; the reference distance 105.5 − 102.6 = 2.9 projects to about 99.7.

Who developed or popularized it?

Classical chart pattern; systematised by Richard Schabacker and by Edwards and Magee

Such patterns were described systematically by Richard Schabacker in the 1930s and popularised by Edwards and Magee’s 1948 “Technical Analysis of Stock Trends”; the earliest users are unverified.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Find three highs and confirm the middle one is highest.
  2. Join the two lows into a neckline (it may slope).
  3. Only a close below the neckline completes the pattern; before that it is a “possible” head and shoulders.
  4. After completion, a close back above the right shoulder invalidates it.

The mistake to avoid

The two most common mistakes are calling the pattern before the right shoulder has finished, and treating the reference distance as a guaranteed target.
