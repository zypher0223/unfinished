Yield Aggregators & Restaking

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

DefinitionYield aggregators are smart contract vaults that automate reward harvesting and compounding across DeFi protocols, while restaking reuses staked assets to secure additional middleware networks for stacked yields.

In DeFi, lending interest, swap fees, and staking rewards are scattered across protocols (see the "DeFi Lending & Liquidation" and "Staking, Liquid Staking & Restaking" lessons on this site). Harvesting and reinvesting rewards manually incurs heavy gas fees for individual users. Yield aggregators solve this by pooling user deposits into automated smart contract vaults that harvest reward tokens, swap them back into the base asset, and compound positions while socializing gas costs across all depositors. Restaking extends capital reuse to the consensus layer: users take staked ETH or Liquid Staking Tokens (LSTs) already securing Ethereum and opt into additional slashing conditions to secure external middleware—known as Actively Validated Services (AVSs), such as oracles, bridges, or data availability layers—often wrapped into Liquid Restaking Tokens (LRTs). However, stacking yields stacks risks: if an operator equivocates or suffers downtime, the same underlying collateral can be slashed across multiple protocols simultaneously; furthermore, smart contract exploits at any nested layer or secondary market LRT depegs can trigger cascading liquidations.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

A yield aggregator is like neighbors pooling funds to hire an automated property manager who collects rent and reinvests it to save everyone individual trip costs; restaking is like pledging the same house deed as collateral to guarantee three separate business contracts—you collect three guarantee fees in calm times, but a single default in any of the three businesses puts your house on the chopping block.

A made-up example

Suppose a user deposits 2 ETH (at $2,500 per ETH, $5,000 principal) into a restaking yield vault earning 3.5% base staking yield plus 2.5% AVS restaking yield. After the aggregator deducts a 10% performance fee, the net annual yield is (3.5% + 2.5%) * (1 - 0.10) = 5.4% ($270 per year). However, if the delegated operator triggers a 10% slashing penalty ($500 loss) on an AVS fault, the penalty wipes out the entire year's yield and leaves a $230 net principal loss (for illustrative purposes only).

Who developed or popularized it?

From Yearn Finance Vaults (2020) to EigenLayer Restaking (2023)

During the 2020 DeFi Summer, Yearn Finance introduced automated yield-compounding vaults (yVaults), followed by multi-chain aggregators like Beefy and Convex. In June 2023, EigenLayer—founded by University of Washington professor Sreeram Kannan—launched its Phase 1 restaking contracts on Ethereum mainnet, pioneering a marketplace for pooled cryptographic security.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Trace the underlying yield source: distinguish organic consensus rewards and swap fees from inflationary token emissions.
  2. Count smart contract nesting layers: map every contract wrapper between your wallet, the LST/LRT, the restaking layer, and the aggregator vault (see the "Smart Contracts & Token Approvals" lesson on this site).
  3. Inspect operator and slashing rules: verify which node operators manage your stake and what fault conditions trigger AVS slashing.
  4. Check withdrawal queues and pool depth: compare native unbonding delays against secondary DEX liquidity depth to gauge depeg risk.
  5. Calculate net returns after fees: deduct vault management fees, performance fees, and withdrawal fees from advertised gross APY.

The mistake to avoid

Misconception 1: Chasing triple-digit advertised APYs funded by hyper-inflationary reward tokens whose price collapse erodes returns. Misconception 2: Treating restaking points and layered yields as risk-free—each added layer compounds smart contract and slashing risk. Misconception 3: Looping LRTs with high borrowing leverage, where a minor secondary market discount triggers instant liquidation.
