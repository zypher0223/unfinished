Gann Theory

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Gann tools measure price and elapsed time together using proportional lines or grids. Begin with the chosen price-per-time unit rather than mysterious dates.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Lay a fixed coordinate grid over the chart and observe which slope price follows.

Who developed or popularized it?

William Delbert Gann

Gann fans and squares are linked to W. D. Gann's price/time work. Historical attribution does not establish the validity of predictive claims.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Fix timeframe, linear/log scale and price units.
  2. Place a Gann Fan at a clear high or low.
  3. Check the actual price/time ratio instead of trusting a visual 45-degree line.
  4. Record reactions with fixed settings and test them without constant refitting.

The mistake to avoid

Zoom and units alter the display. Angles and cycle stories do not guarantee future turns.

A concrete practice sequence

  1. Fix the price/time scale before selecting Gann Fan or Gann Box and anchoring it to a preselected turn.
  2. Record price per time unit and whether the scale is linear or logarithmic. A visual 45-degree line after zooming is not a market law.
