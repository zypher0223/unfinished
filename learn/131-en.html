Breakouts & Failed Breakouts

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Price spends a long time inside a box; when a bar finally closes outside it, that is a breakout. If price closes back inside the box within a few bars, it is a failed breakout: nobody outside the box followed through.

DefinitionA breakout is a bar that closes beyond the prior range high (or low). A failed breakout is one where, within the next 1–3 bars, price closes back inside the range. A wick that pokes through without a close beyond the level is not counted as a breakout here.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like stepping out of a door and stepping straight back in because no one outside was waiting.

A made-up example

Illustration only: the range high is 102.00. A bar closes at 102.60, beyond 102.00, so it is a breakout; the next bar closes at 101.70, back below 102.00, so the breakout failed after one bar.

Who developed or popularized it?

Taught systematically by Al Brooks and other price-action educators; no single inventor verified

Breakouts and ranges are basic ideas of classical technical analysis; systematic treatment of failed breakouts and follow-through is common in price-action courses such as Al Brooks’s.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Draw the range boundaries from the highs and lows of the bars inside it.
  2. Wait for a bar to close: only a close beyond the boundary is a breakout, not a wick.
  3. Watch the next 1–3 closes: the breakout holds only if they stay outside, ideally with a follow-through bar.
  4. Treat a close back inside the range as a failed breakout and watch whether price heads to the other side.

The mistake to avoid

The classic mistake is treating an intrabar poke through the boundary as a breakout and chasing it; many such moves are back inside by the close.
