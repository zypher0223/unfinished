Hammer & Hanging Man

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

DefinitionShape: a lower shadow at least twice the body, a short upper shadow (no longer than the body), and the body in the upper part of the range. The same shape is called a hammer after a decline and a hanging man after a rise; the name depends on location, not colour.

The hammer and the hanging man look identical: a small body with a long lower shadow. Only the location differs: after a decline it is a hammer, after a rise a hanging man. Both need the next bar to confirm.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

The same umbrella is a sunshade on a sunny day and a rain umbrella in the rain; it depends on the weather.

A made-up example

Illustration only: open 50.0, close 50.2, high 50.25, low 49.4. The body is 0.2, the lower shadow 0.6 is three times the body, and the 0.05 upper shadow is short. After a decline this is a hammer.

Who developed or popularized it?

Japanese candlestick tradition; popularised in the West by Steve Nison

Candlestick patterns come from Japanese rice-trading tradition; the specific originator is unverified (often attributed to Munehisa Homma without reliable primary sources). Steve Nison’s 1991 “Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques” popularised them in the West.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Measure the body and both shadows and check the ratios.
  2. Look at the preceding move: after a decline it is a hammer, after a rise a hanging man.
  3. Note the bar’s low (hammer) or its close (hanging man) as the reference.
  4. Wait for the next bar: a higher close after a hammer is follow-through; a break of its low invalidates it.

The mistake to avoid

The most common confusion is recognising only the shape and ignoring location, calling a bar in the middle of a rally a bullish hammer.
