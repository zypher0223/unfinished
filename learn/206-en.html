DeFi Lending & Liquidation

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

DefinitionDecentralized lending protocols such as Aave and Compound let users deposit assets to earn interest or borrow other assets against deposits. Loans are usually overcollateralized: borrowing capacity is collateral value times the loan-to-value (LTV) ratio. Health factor = collateral value × liquidation threshold ÷ debt value; below 1, anyone may repay part of your debt and take your collateral at a discount, which is liquidation.

On-chain lending ignores credit history and looks only at collateral; the rules are in code, execute automatically, and nobody calls you to top up. A falling collateral price or a rising borrowed-asset price both cut the health factor, and once it drops below 1 you are liquidated and pay a penalty on top.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like pawning gold for a loan: the pawnshop watches only the gold price, and once it falls far enough it automatically sells part of your gold to repay the debt, at a discount.

A made-up example

Illustration only: you deposit 10 ETH at USD 2,000 (USD 20,000 total), with an 80% liquidation threshold, and borrow 10,000 USDC. Health factor = 20,000 × 0.8 ÷ 10,000 = 1.6. If ETH falls to USD 1,250, collateral is worth USD 12,500 and the health factor = 12,500 × 0.8 ÷ 10,000 = 1.0, so liquidation begins; a liquidator repays part of your debt and takes the matching ETH at about a 5% discount.

Who developed or popularized it?

MakerDAO (DAI, 2017) pioneered overcollateralized lending; Compound (2018) and Aave (relaunched from ETHLend in 2020) popularised pooled lending

MakerDAO launched DAI, a stablecoin minted against overcollateralized ETH, in December 2017. Compound launched pooled lending in 2018 and its COMP token in June 2020, sparking the “liquidity mining” boom (DeFi Summer). In the 12 March 2020 crash, Ethereum congestion let some MakerDAO liquidation auctions clear near zero, a key lesson for liquidation design.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Before borrowing, work out the liquidation price: where the health factor equals 1.
  2. Keep the health factor well above 1 to leave room for sharp moves.
  3. Set price alerts and add collateral or repay part of the loan early if needed.
  4. Learn the protocol’s oracle and liquidation penalty, and avoid large loans against illiquid assets.

The mistake to avoid

Depositing just because the rate is high, while ignoring contract bugs, oracle failures and de-pegging. A high rate often signals higher risk or thin demand.
