Blockchain & the Trilemma

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

A traditional transfer relies on one bank keeping the ledger; a blockchain lets thousands of computers each keep the same ledger and confirm new transactions by shared rules. No single party can quietly edit the books or shut the service down, but because many parties must confirm every transaction, speed and cost rarely match a centralized system.

DefinitionA blockchain is a ledger kept by many nodes and chained in time order: transactions from a period are packed into a “block”, and each block carries the hash (data fingerprint) of the previous one, so altering any page breaks every fingerprint after it. The “trilemma” says decentralization, security and scalability are hard to maximize at the same time; every chain trades among the three.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

A village used to have one ledger kept by the headman; if he erred or was bribed, everyone suffered. Now every household holds an identical copy, and anyone who edits their own copy is caught the moment it is compared with the others.

A made-up example

Illustration only: one chain lets only 100 large nodes produce blocks and handles thousands of transactions per second, but ordinary users can hardly run a node, so it is less decentralized; another lets anyone validate from a home computer and handles a dozen or so per second. Neither is simply better; they sit at different points of the trilemma.

Who developed or popularized it?

Satoshi Nakamoto’s Bitcoin white paper (2008); the “trilemma” popularised by Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin

On 31 October 2008 the pseudonymous Satoshi Nakamoto published the Bitcoin white paper, combining a timestamped hash chain with proof of work to solve double spending without a central party; the genesis block followed on 3 January 2009. The term “blockchain trilemma” came into wide use around 2017 through Vitalik Buterin and others discussing Ethereum scaling.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Separate on-chain records from exchange balances; the latter are just the exchange’s internal bookkeeping.
  2. For any chain ask: who can validate, what security incidents has it had, how many transactions per second and at what fee.
  3. Put “fast and cheap” claims back into the trilemma: which side is being given up?
  4. Know what hashing and chaining do: they make history hard to rewrite quietly, but cannot prove that what was written is true.

The mistake to avoid

Believing “on-chain means true”. A blockchain only makes records hard to alter after they are written; it does not make a project’s claims true or give a token value.
