The Doji Family

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

DefinitionA doji has a body smaller than 10% of its range. A gravestone doji has open and close within the lowest 10% of the range (long upper shadow); a dragonfly doji has them within the highest 10% (long lower shadow); a long-legged doji has long shadows on both sides with the body in the middle. All three show indecision, not reversal.

Doji have opens and closes that are almost equal. A gravestone looks like an upside-down T, a dragonfly like a T, and a long-legged doji is long at both ends. They show a stand-off between buyers and sellers; the direction depends on what follows.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like a long tug-of-war in which the rope ends up back at the start; who wins is decided in the next round.

A made-up example

Illustration only: open 60.00, close 60.02, high 60.05, low 59.20. The 0.02 body is less than 10% of the 0.85 range, and open and close are in the top 10% of the range: a dragonfly doji.

Who developed or popularized it?

Japanese candlestick tradition; popularised in the West by Steve Nison

Candlestick patterns come from Japanese rice-trading tradition; the specific originator is unverified (often attributed to Munehisa Homma without reliable primary sources). Steve Nison’s 1991 “Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques” popularised them in the West.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Compute the body as a share of the range and confirm it is under 10%.
  2. Check where open and close sit: near the bottom is a gravestone, near the top a dragonfly, centred with long shadows a long-legged doji.
  3. Consider location: end of a trend or middle of a range.
  4. Wait for later closes; without directional confirmation, keep treating it as indecision.

The mistake to avoid

Treating a gravestone as a sell signal or a dragonfly as a buy signal is the most common misuse; they only show indecision.
