Aggregators & Launchpads

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

On-chain liquidity for any given token is often fragmented across dozens of decentralized exchanges (see the "DEXs & AMMs" lesson on this site). Executing a large swap inside a single pool causes severe slippage and price impact (see the "Liquidity & Market Impact" lesson on this site). DEX aggregators solve this without maintaining their own pools: their routing algorithms scan on-chain pools and private market makers (RFQs) in real time, splitting a single order across multiple venues to maximize net output after gas fees. Upstream of secondary trading, Launchpads (Initial DEX Offerings or IDOs) handle primary token distribution, typically requiring users to stake native platform tokens or complete tasks to earn lottery allocation tickets. Key failure modes in this sector center on token launches: first, the platform tokens locked to qualify for an IDO often drop in value by more than the profit from the small allocation; second, many launchpad tokens debut with low float and high Fully Diluted Valuation (FDV) (see the "Tokenomics: Supply, FDV & Unlocks" lesson on this site), suffering heavy sell pressure as vesting cliffs unlock; third, permissionless launchpads frequently host unvetted projects that drain liquidity pools (rug pulls) shortly after listing.

DefinitionDEX aggregators are routing protocols that split orders across multiple liquidity venues to minimize slippage and execution costs, while Launchpads (IDOs) facilitate early public token sales and fundraising on-chain.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

A DEX aggregator acts like a flight meta-search engine that owns no airplanes but splits a group booking across three airlines to secure the lowest total fare; a Launchpad is like a store requiring you to buy and lock an expensive VIP membership card just to enter a raffle for a discounted item—if the VIP card itself plunges in value or the item is quickly mass-produced, winning the raffle can still leave you at a net loss.

A made-up example

Suppose swapping 100,000 USDC for ETH in Pool A alone yields 39.2 ETH due to price impact; a DEX aggregator splits 60,000 USDC through Pool A and 40,000 USDC through Pool B, yielding 39.8 ETH after an extra $8 in multi-hop gas—a net improvement of ~0.6 ETH. Separately, suppose a user buys and locks $2,000 of a launchpad's token to win a $100 IDO allocation: even if the new token nets an $80 gain, a 15% drop in the locked launchpad token ($300 loss) results in a net loss of $300 - $80 = $220 (for illustrative purposes only).

Who developed or popularized it?

The Birth of 1inch at ETHNewYork (2019) and the Evolution of IDO Launchpads

In May 2019, developers Sergej Kunz and Anton Bukov built the first DEX aggregator, 1inch, at the ETHNewYork hackathon, followed by 0x API (Matcha), ParaSwap, and meta-aggregators like DefiLlama Swap. On the primary issuance side, following 2017 ICOs and 2019 centralized exchange IEOs, Initial DEX Offerings (IDOs) emerged between 2019 and 2021 as an on-chain token launch mechanism.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Compare net output after gas: verify that a multi-hop aggregator route still beats a direct single-pool swap after accounting for higher gas usage.
  2. Verify router contract approvals: grant token allowances only to verified router contracts with bounded amounts (see the "Smart Contracts & Token Approvals" lesson on this site).
  3. Account for platform token exposure: weigh the price risk or hedging cost of staking launchpad tokens against the expected size of the IDO allocation.
  4. Inspect tokenomics and vesting schedules: check initial circulating supply, FDV, and TGE/cliff unlock dates for early investors.
  5. Check LP lock status on permissionless launches: confirm whether initial liquidity tokens are locked in a verified timelock contract.

The mistake to avoid

Misconception 1: Assuming multi-pool splitting always saves money on tiny swaps—extra multi-hop gas fees on Mainnet can exceed the tiny slippage saved. Misconception 2: Staking thousands of dollars in a volatile launchpad token unhedged just to receive a tiny lottery allocation. Misconception 3: Market-buying a newly listed token in the first block when sniper bots and early unlocks dump into shallow liquidity.
