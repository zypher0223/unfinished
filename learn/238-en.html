Lightning Network & Ordinals

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Bitcoin blocks have limited space, and on-chain transfers pay fees and wait for confirmation, which does not suit small payments like a coffee. Lightning works like opening a tab and settling at the end: you can pay no more through a channel than you locked in, and payments may be routed through other people’s channels, so the path needs enough balance. Many people use custodial Lightning wallets, where a provider manages the channels and keys; that is convenient but requires trust. Inscriptions take a different path: they make Bitcoin transactions carry content, and when inscriptions are busy they compete with ordinary transfers for block space and push fees up. Inscriptions share the risks of NFTs: copies, thin liquidity and prices set by a few buyers.

DefinitionThe Lightning Network is a network of payment channels built on Bitcoin: two parties lock bitcoin into a channel, later payments only update their balances off-chain, and only opening and closing the channel touch the chain, so small, frequent payments are faster and cheaper. It was proposed in a 2016 draft paper by Joseph Poon and Thaddeus Dryja, and its specifications are called BOLTs. Ordinals number every satoshi and inscriptions write content such as text or images into Bitcoin transactions tied to a particular satoshi, often called “NFTs on Bitcoin”.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

A Lightning channel is like opening a tab at your regular café and settling once at the end; an inscription is like engraving a coin: it is still the same coin, now carrying some content.

A made-up example

Illustration only: Wang opens a channel with a café and locks in 100,000 sats (1 bitcoin = 100,000,000 sats). He buys 5 coffees at 8,000 sats each, so the off-chain balances shift by 5 × 8,000 = 40,000: Wang has 100,000 − 40,000 = 60,000 sats and the café 40,000. Only two on-chain transactions occur, opening and closing; at 2,000 sats each (illustration only), that is 2 × 2,000 = 4,000 sats. Paying all 5 on-chain would cost 5 × 2,000 = 10,000 sats, or 10,000 − 4,000 = 6,000 more.

Who developed or popularized it?

Lightning: Joseph Poon and Thaddeus Dryja (2016 draft paper); inscriptions: Ordinal Theory, handbook by Casey Rodarmor and others

The draft of “The Bitcoin Lightning Network: Scalable Off-Chain Instant Payments” is dated 14 January 2016; Lightning’s specifications, the BOLTs (Basis of Lightning Technology), are still being revised. The Ordinal Theory handbook explains that individual satoshis can be inscribed with arbitrary content and transferred with Bitcoin transactions.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Know your wallet type: a custodial Lightning wallet means the provider holds the funds; self-custody means managing channels and backups yourself.
  2. Match the method to the amount: Lightning for small, frequent payments, an on-chain Bitcoin address for large long-term holdings.
  3. Before paying or receiving, check the amount and expiry on the Lightning invoice, and do not pay an expired one.
  4. Before buying an inscription, use the NFT lesson’s checks: the ID, where the content lives and real bids, not just listing prices.

The mistake to avoid

Using Lightning to store large amounts of bitcoin, or thinking inscriptions are a new asset issued by Bitcoin itself. Lightning suits small payments; inscriptions are a third-party protocol whose value depends entirely on buyers and sellers.
