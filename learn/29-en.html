A beginner’s reading guide · Beginner

ADR

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

ADR (Average Daily Range, an indicator averaging each day's intraday High-minus-Low range over the past N trading days) asks how wide the typical intraday high-to-low span has been over the last 14 days, excluding overnight gaps between yesterday's close and today's range. It displays in price units (or percentage in ADR% variants). Beginners often treat 1x ADR as a hard daily ceiling that price cannot pass.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

It averages the widest distance you walked across your yard each day over the past two weeks without counting overnight travel between yards; however, averaging 5 meters a day does not stop you from walking 12 meters on a busy day.

A made-up example

Illustration only: N-day ADR = ∑(Daily High - Daily Low) / N. Over 3 days, suppose Day 1 has High 104 and Low 100 (intraday range = 104 - 100 = 4); Day 2 gaps up with High 112 and Low 106 (intraday range = 112 - 106 = 6, excluding the overnight gap that ATR would count); Day 3 has High 110 and Low 105 (range = 110 - 105 = 5). Then 3-day ADR = (4 + 6 + 5) / 3 = 15 / 3 = 5. If Day 4 has moved 4 points so far (4 / 5 = 80% of ADR), it shows typical range usage, not a hard cap.

First: why look at this indicator?

Average Daily Range (ADR) calculates (High − Low) for each of the past N days and averages those daily high-low spans, describing 'how far price typically travels between the day's high and low within a single session.' Because it usually excludes overnight gaps from yesterday's close to today's open, it differs from ATR. It offers a historical reference for intraday range size—not a daily quota that price is required to fill.

When it is most useful

Use ADR when you want to see whether today's intraday high-low span is still quiet or already stretched compared with recent days. On stocks that frequently gap overnight, always check ATR or yesterday's close alongside ADR; otherwise a narrow intraday high-low range can hide a massive overnight gap.

Step 1: where exactly do you look?

Find the location first, then the units. Colours differ between platforms, so rely on names and meanings rather than colours.

The lower ADR curve or on-chart numeric label
A 14-day ADR of 5 means the intraday high-minus-low averaged about 5 price units over the past 14 days, not 5%. Some variants express ADR in percent (ADR%), so check the label; if a script simply averages the last 14 bars on a 5-minute chart, do not call that a 'daily' range.
Today's session high, session low, and daily session dividers on the main chart
Subtract today's low from today's high and compare against ADR. When viewing an intraday chart, confirm the indicator still pulls daily high-low data rather than averaging 14 five-minute bars.

Step 2: what does a change mean?

You see: Today's high-low range so far is much smaller than the ADR

What it suggests: Intraday movement up to this point is smaller than the recent daily average—either because the session is still early or because trading is quiet.

What it does not tell you yet: It does not mean price is obligated to travel the remaining distance before the close.

You see: Today's high-low range has already exceeded the ADR

What it suggests: Intraday movement today is wider than the recent daily average.

What it does not tell you yet: It does not assure price will stop moving or reverse immediately.

You see: ADR has been declining, yet overnight gaps are large

What it suggests: Quieter intraday ranges do not mean total holding risk has fallen.

What it does not tell you yet: Never use a low ADR to dismiss overnight gap risk.

The easiest mistake to make

Treating an average (1x ADR) as a daily maximum limit and fading a strong trend day just because price already traveled 1x ADR from the morning low; loading a basic High-minus-Low average on a 5-minute chart without a daily timeframe lock and mistaking 5-minute range for daily range.

Step 3: read one chart together

This is a made-up reading exercise, not live market data. The point is to move from what you see to a reasonable explanation.

Hypothetical past three days with daily High/Low pairs of 104/100, 108/102, and 110/105 yuan—intraday ranges of 4, 6, and 5 yuan, giving a 3-day ADR of (4 + 6 + 5) ÷ 3 = 5 yuan.

  1. Today's session prints a high of 113 and a low of 110 so far—an intraday range of 3 yuan, smaller than the 5-yuan ADR.
  2. Even if yesterday closed at 100 and today opened at 110, that 10-yuan overnight gap is not counted inside today's 3-yuan (113 − 110) intraday range.
  3. In the afternoon, the low extends down to 106, widening today's range to 113 − 106 = 7 yuan (above the 5-yuan ADR); the average does not block price at 5 yuan.

Putting the observations together, you could say

ADR describes intrabar session span and cannot replace total change measured from yesterday's close; exceeding the average simply means today is more active than usual.

But do not jump to this conclusion: It cannot tell how much further price will move this afternoon, nor does fading a move just because it reached 1× ADR assure a profit.

One last question: did it click?

If the ADR is 5 yuan and price has already spanned 5 yuan between today's low and high, can you conclude it will not make a new high or low for the rest of the day?

Think first, then open the answer

No. An average is a historical summary, not a price-limit rule. A given day might span 2 yuan or 15 yuan; treating the average as a hard wall misunderstands what ADR measures.

Once you can say “what I see → what it means → what is still uncertain”, practise with the platform walkthrough below.

TradingView

Search terms：Average Daily Range

Official built-in ADR; check its calculation timeframe on intraday charts. Practice setup: Add Average Daily Range on a daily chart with Length 14.

  1. Open the TradingView chart using the button below. Choose a stock or coin at the upper left and check the exchange. Choose a chart interval, for example 1 hour or 1 day; one daily candle represents one day.
  2. Use Indicators in the upper chart toolbar, then enter one of the search terms below. Check built-in Technicals first. Community scripts with similar names may calculate different things.
  3. Select the matching result to add it, close the search, and look for a new line or lower pane. If there is no exact built-in match, read the availability note rather than substituting a different indicator.
  4. Hover over the added indicator name and open Settings. Inputs changes calculation settings such as length; start with the defaults. Style changes visual appearance. Follow this lesson’s interpretation steps.
  5. Review completed historical candles and explain what the indicator showed at that time. Adding it to a chart does not place a trade. Practise reading it before any simulated execution.

AICoin

Search terms：ADR / 平均每日振幅

Standalone entry unverified. ADR can also name an unrelated advances/declines ratio.

  1. Open the AICoin web chart below. Select an exchange and pair, such as BTC/USDT. Check whether you selected spot or a perpetual contract: their data can differ.
  2. Look for the chart-top Indicators button, labelled 指标 in Chinese. Search the abbreviation below first, then try the other terms. Common indicators may also appear in the shortcut bar below the chart.
  3. Add only an exact matching result. Check whether it appears over price or in a separate pane. Similar names do not guarantee the same formula.
  4. If the implementation exposes parameters/settings, open them and start with the defaults, then follow the interpretation steps on this page. Check the official help if your interface differs.
  5. No result may mean the feature is unavailable in your version. Check existing Custom indicators (自定义指标) only if you can verify their formula. Otherwise learn it on a platform that supports it; do not assume every indicator here is built in.
