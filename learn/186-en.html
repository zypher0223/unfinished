Earnings Season & Guidance

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Many beginners believe profitable earnings automatically push stock prices higher, but markets trade on expectations. Even robust profits will trigger sharp selloffs and price gaps if results lag analyst consensus or management downgrades future guidance.

DefinitionEarnings season is the concentrated period when public companies report periodic financials; Consensus Estimates represent the mean analyst forecast for revenue and EPS; Guidance is management's official forward-looking performance forecast. Share price revaluation around earnings depends primarily on the deviation between actual figures and forward guidance relative to market expectations.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Earnings are like an exam where expectations are set at 95; scoring a 90 is viewed by the market as a disappointment.

A made-up example

Illustration only: A company reports Q3 EPS of $1.20, beating the $1.10 consensus estimate. However, management slashes next quarter's revenue guidance from $1.5 billion to $1.2 billion. The stock gaps down from $100 to $92 (-8%) at the market open. This demonstrates that forward-looking guidance carries far more weight in equity valuation than past reported profits.

Who developed or popularized it?

Financial Analyst Consensus and SEC Regulation FD

In 1971 Lynch, Jones & Ryan launched I/B/E/S, which began compiling analysts' earnings forecasts into consensus estimates; First Call, founded in 1984, added real-time aggregation. In 2000 the SEC adopted Regulation FD (Fair Disclosure), barring companies from selectively disclosing material information to particular analysts or investors, so guidance must be released to the whole market at once.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Verify whether the earnings announcement is scheduled Before Market Open (BMO) or After Market Close (AMC) on official calendars.
  2. Identify the consensus analyst benchmarks for EPS and revenue across platforms like FactSet or Bloomberg.
  3. Calculate the earnings surprise percentage immediately following the headline disclosure.
  4. Scrutinize management forward guidance and the earnings call conference transcript for qualitative outlook changes.
  5. Watch out for 'sell the news' reactions where strong earnings are met with heavy profit-taking following a prolonged pre-earnings runup.

The mistake to avoid

Assuming past earnings growth guarantees an immediate stock rally, ignoring that forward guidance revisions dictate valuation multiples.
