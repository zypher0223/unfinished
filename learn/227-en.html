Concentrated Liquidity & Liquidity Management

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

In early automated market makers like Uniswap v2, liquidity providers (LPs) spread their capital uniformly across the entire price curve from zero to infinity (see the "DEXs & AMMs" lesson on this site), leaving most capital idle far from the current market price. Concentrated liquidity (introduced in Uniswap v3) allows LPs to allocate capital within custom price ranges defined by discrete ticks (for example, providing ETH/USDC liquidity strictly between $1,800 and $2,200). As long as the market price stays inside that active range, an LP can provide the same market depth and earn the same fee share with a fraction of the capital, with each custom position represented as a unique ERC-721 NFT. However, higher capital efficiency comes with sharp trade-offs: the moment the market price moves below the lower bound or above the upper bound, the position converts 100% into the less valuable asset, stops earning swap fees entirely, and experiences amplified Impermanent Loss per dollar deployed. Furthermore, actively rebalancing ranges incurs swap slippage, gas fees, and the risk of locking in permanent losses during whipsaw volatility.

DefinitionAn automated market maker mechanism allowing liquidity providers (LPs) to allocate capital within custom price ranges (ticks) for higher capital efficiency, alongside active range-management strategies.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Full-range liquidity is like setting up water stands every mile along a 500-mile highway where most stands sit empty; concentrated liquidity packs all your inventory right at the busiest rest stop—earning far more sales per dollar of stock, until traffic detours onto another road (price leaves your range), leaving your stand with zero customers unless you pay to haul everything to the new route.

A made-up example

Suppose ETH trades at 2,000 USDC. LP A deposits 5 ETH + 10,000 USDC ($20,000) across the full range; LP B deposits about 1 ETH + 2,000 USDC ($4,000) in the 1,600–2,500 range. Using the Uniswap v3 liquidity formula, B's liquidity is about 1.9 times A's, so while the price stays in range B earns about 1.9 times A's fees with one fifth of the capital — roughly 9.5 times the efficiency per dollar. If ETH falls to $1,500, below the range, B's position turns entirely into about 2.12 ETH (worth about $3,177) and stops earning fees; simply holding the original 1 ETH and 2,000 USDC would be worth about $3,500. Withdrawing and swapping back to USDC at this point turns the paper impermanent loss into a realised loss (for illustrative purposes only).

Who developed or popularized it?

Uniswap v3's Introduction of Concentrated Liquidity (May 2021) and v4 Hooks (2025)

Uniswap Labs published the v3 whitepaper in March 2021 and launched on Ethereum mainnet in May 2021, introducing discrete price ticks and concentrated liquidity represented by ERC-721 NFTs (an official Final Ethereum standard). In January 2025, Uniswap v4 retained concentrated liquidity while introducing a singleton contract architecture and customizable Hooks.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Match range width to pair volatility: use tight ticks for correlated pairs (like stablecoins) and wider ranges for volatile pairs.
  2. Select the appropriate fee tier: choose 0.01%–0.05% for stable pairs, 0.30% for major pairs, and 1.00% for exotic volatile tokens.
  3. Monitor out-of-range asset conversion: recognize that concentrated LP positions behave similarly to short options as prices approach range boundaries (see the "Covered Calls & Protective Puts" lesson on this site).
  4. Weigh rebalancing costs against fee income: calculate swap costs, gas fees, and realized divergence loss before shifting ranges.
  5. Vet automated liquidity managers (ALMs) carefully: check historical drawdown and rebalance decay before depositing into automated vaults.

The mistake to avoid

Misconception 1: Setting ultra-narrow ranges on volatile tokens for high headline APRs—prices quickly exit the range, halting fees and magnifying impermanent loss. Misconception 2: Compulsively rebalancing every time price leaves the range, locking in permanent losses during choppy markets. Misconception 3: Assuming Automated Liquidity Management (ALM) vaults guarantee profits—automated rebalancing often underperforms during sharp V-shaped reversals.
