Elliott Wave Theory

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Elliott waves classify moves as trend-following impulses and countertrend corrections. The basic illustration uses five impulse waves and three corrective waves, but real charts are less tidy.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

A climb has pushes and rests, but the steps do not always fit a neat count.

Who developed or popularized it?

Ralph Nelson Elliott

Elliott developed the Wave Principle in the 1930s and published The Wave Principle in 1938; Frost and Prechter later popularized it.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Learn impulse rules before merely memorizing five up and three down.
  2. Use one timeframe and label clear turns with wave drawings.
  3. Write an invalidation price; in a standard impulse wave 2 cannot pass wave 1's starting point.
  4. Keep an alternative count and let subsequent price reject an idea.

The mistake to avoid

Wave counts are subjective and easily revised after the event. Attractive labels do not prove forecasting skill.

  1. Find Elliott Impulse Wave (12345) in the pattern drawings or use paths and text. Click completed turns in order and label corrections separately.
  2. Save the initial count and invalidation price before revealing subsequent bars. Reject a count that breaks its rules and keep a record instead of silently relabeling history.
