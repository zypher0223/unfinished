Cup and Handle

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

DefinitionCup and handle: price falls from a high (the rim), forms a rounded bottom (the cup, not a V), and returns near the rim; it then pulls back slightly to form a “handle” whose retracement is less than half the cup’s depth. A close above the rim and handle high is the breakout. William O’Neil described the pattern in his CAN SLIM method and stressed rising volume on the breakout.

Cup and handle looks like a coffee cup seen from the side: a rounded cup with a small handle on the right. It describes price slowly basing, returning to the old high, pausing briefly and breaking out. It has many elements; be cautious if any one is off.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like the side view of a teacup: a rounded bottom, a rim and a small handle.

A made-up example

Illustration only: the rim is 104.0 and the cup bottom 100.5 (3.5 deep). After returning to 103.9, the handle dips to 103.0 (0.9, less than half of 3.5). Only a close above 104.0 is a breakout.

Who developed or popularized it?

William O’Neil (described in the CAN SLIM method)

William O’Neil described the cup with handle systematically in “How to Make Money in Stocks” (1988); rounded-base consolidations were discussed earlier, and whether he was first is unverified.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Find the rim and the cup bottom and confirm the bottom is rounded, not a V.
  2. Check that price returns near the original rim.
  3. Check that the handle retraces less than half the cup’s depth.
  4. A close above the rim is the breakout, ideally on higher volume; a close back below the rim afterwards invalidates it.

The mistake to avoid

Drawing a sharp V-shaped drop and recovery as a cup, or ignoring an overly deep handle, strips the pattern of its meaning.
