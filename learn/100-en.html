TPO/Market Profile

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

TPO / Market Profile asks where price spent the most time during a session versus where it visited only briefly. It plots letter profiles on the main chart, measuring time spent at price rather than traded volume. Beginners often confuse the widest row of TPO letters with the highest-volume price.

Profile Time Price Opportunitya charting method that divides each session into fixed time blocks—typically 30-minute periods labeled A, B, C—and stacks a letter at every price row visited during that block
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

It is like an attendance check every 30 minutes on each floor of a department store: four letters on Floor 2 mean shoppers were present during four half-hour checks, without counting how many items they actually bought.

A made-up example

Illustration only: across a 2-hour session split into four 30-minute blocks (A, B, C, D) and 1-point price rows (100, 101, 102, 103), suppose Period A visits 100–101, Period B visits 101–102, Period C visits 101–103, and Period D visits 101–102. Stacking letters left-aligned gives: 100 [A] (1), 101 [A,B,C,D] (4), 102 [B,C,D] (3), and 103 [C] (1), totaling 1 + 4 + 3 + 1 = 9 TPOs. Row 101 is widest (4 TPOs = TPO POC), and rows 101–102 hold 7 / 9 ≈ 77.8% (≥ 70%) for the Value Area. This measures time residency, not volume.

First: why look at this tool?

TPO slices each session into fixed time blocks (such as 30-minute periods labeled A, B, C) and records which price rows were visited in each block, helping you answer where price spent the most time versus where it passed through briefly. It measures time spent at price, not traded volume.

TPO Time Price Opportunityor Market Profile

When it is most useful

Ideal for distinguishing balanced, rotational sessions (bell-shaped profiles) from directional trend sessions (elongated profiles). Because TPO counts time blocks rather than shares or contracts, do not use it alone when you need to know how much volume traded on a sudden spike.

Step 1: where exactly do you look?

Find the location first, then the units. Colours differ between platforms, so rely on names and meanings rather than colours.

Letter matrix (or block profile) and TPO Point of Control (POC) on the chart
Each horizontal row is a price step, and each letter shows that a 30-minute period touched that price; the widest row of letters marks the time-based POC.
TPO Value Area (VAH/VAL) and Initial Balance (IB) range
The price band containing roughly 70% of the session's letters forms the Value Area, while the high-low span of the first two periods (A and B) marks the Initial Balance.

Step 2: what does a change mean?

You see: Letters stack wide around the middle price rows and taper at both ends in a bell shape

What it suggests: Price rotated repeatedly across the middle zone through most of the session's time blocks.

What it does not tell you yet: It does not ensure the next session will stay inside the same range or that volume matched time.

You see: The profile stretches vertically with single-letter rows ('single prints') along the move

What it suggests: Price traveled rapidly in one direction, visiting those price rows in only a single 30-minute block.

What it does not tell you yet: It does not mean single-print zones can never be traded through later.

You see: Only one letter touches the session high before subsequent letters build lower

What it suggests: Price spent very little time at the top extreme before rotating back down.

What it does not tell you yet: A brief time visit alone does not ensure a major multi-week top.

The easiest mistake to make

Assuming more TPO letters automatically mean more traded volume—six quiet lunchtime hours at 101 print 12 letters on tiny volume, while one explosive 30-minute breakout at 105 prints just 1 letter on massive volume; ignoring how tick-size grouping and session timezone settings reshape the profile.

Step 3: read one chart together

This is a made-up reading exercise, not live market data. The point is to move from what you see to a reasonable explanation.

Illustration only: consider a 2-hour session split into four 30-minute blocks (A, B, C, D) across four price levels: 100, 101, 102, and 103.

  1. Period A trades at 100–101; Period B at 101–102; Period C at 101–103; Period D at 101–102.
  2. Counting letters per row: 100 has [A] (1); 101 has [A,B,C,D] (4); 102 has [B,C,D] (3); 103 has [C] (1), for a total of 1 + 4 + 3 + 1 = 9 TPOs.
  3. Price 101 has the widest row (4 letters), making 101 the TPO POC; rows 101 and 102 hold 4 + 3 = 7 of 9 letters (77.8% > 70%), forming the Value Area.

Putting the observations together, you could say

Price spent the most time between 101 and 102 (visiting 101 in all 4 periods), while touching 100 and 103 in only 1 period each.

But do not jump to this conclusion: It does not prove that 101 had more traded volume than 103, since a huge burst of volume could have occurred during Period C at 103.

One last question: did it click?

On today's TPO chart, price 105 has 5 letters (A through E) while price 108 has only 1 letter (F). Does this prove 5 times more volume traded at 105 than at 108?

Think first, then open the answer

No. TPO counts how many 30-minute time blocks visited a price, not how many contracts traded; a heavy burst of volume during period F at 108 can exceed five quiet periods at 105.

A concrete practice sequence

  1. Open the chart-type menu and look for Time Price Opportunity. It is a chart type rather than an ordinary indicator line; Volume Profile is not an equivalent fallback.
  2. Check session, time zone, time block and row size in settings. Count the blocks visiting one price row, then compare two sessions without changing those settings.
