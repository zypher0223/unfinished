Funding Rate

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Funding Rate (the periodic payment percentage exchanged directly between long and short holders of a perpetual contract, commonly every 8 hours) asks whether the perpetual contract is trading at a premium or discount to spot and which side pays to hold across the settlement timestamp. It plots around 0% in a lower pane. Beginners often calculate funding on their small margin deposit rather than full leveraged notional value, or assume positive funding ensures an immediate crash.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

To keep the perpetual boat from drifting too far from the spot boat, a tethering fee is exchanged every 8 hours: when the perpetual trades above spot (positive rate), longs pay shorts; when it trades below spot (negative rate), shorts pay longs.

A made-up example

Illustration only: Funding Payment = Position Notional Value × Funding Rate. Suppose you post $200 margin at 5x leverage for a long perpetual position, giving a full notional value of $200 × 5 = $1,000. If the settled funding rate at the 16:00 timestamp is +0.05%, under standard rules you pay $1,000 × 0.05% = $0.50 (five times the $0.10 you would get if you multiplied $200 margin by 0.05%). Across three +0.05% settlements in one day, total funding is $0.50 × 3 = $1.50, or $1.50 / $200 = 0.75% of your margin per day. Leverage magnifies carrying cost, though positive funding does not force an immediate price drop.

Who developed or popularized it?

Modern crypto perpetual mechanism popularized by the BitMEX team; no verified sole originator

BitMEX documents its 2016 perpetual product and funding mechanism. Earlier perpetual-futures ideas and other implementations mean this is not proof of one person's invention of all funding rates.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

First: why look at this tool?

The Funding Rate shows the periodic fee percentage exchanged between long and short perpetual contract holders (commonly every 8 hours), helping you answer whether the perpetual trades rich or cheap relative to spot and which side pays to hold positions across the settlement timestamp. Under standard rules, longs pay shorts when funding is positive, and shorts pay longs when negative.

When it is most useful

Essential when holding perpetual contracts across multiple settlement intervals to calculate carrying cost, or when checking whether perpetual pricing is stretched relative to spot. It does not directly charge traders who trade pure spot or close perpetual positions before the funding timestamp.

Step 1: where exactly do you look?

Find the location first, then the units. Colours differ between platforms, so rely on names and meanings rather than colours.

Contract header 'Funding / Countdown' display and lower-pane Funding Rate history
The header shows the current or estimated rate (such as +0.0100%) and countdown timer to settlement, while the lower pane plots historical settled rates around zero.
Position 'Notional Value' field in the trading panel
Funding payments are calculated by multiplying the full leveraged position notional value—not just initial margin—by the funding rate.

Step 2: what does a change mean?

You see: Funding sits steadily near the exchange's baseline rate (such as +0.01% per 8 hours)

What it suggests: Perpetual prices are tracking spot closely with normal baseline cost.

What it does not tell you yet: It does not mean price will stay flat or that holding a leveraged long is free.

You see: Funding spikes to +0.10% or higher during a rapid price rally

What it suggests: The perpetual is trading at a noticeable premium to spot, making longs pay shorts a steep fee at settlement.

What it does not tell you yet: It does not ensure an immediate price crash, as elevated funding can persist during strong rallies.

You see: Funding drops below zero (such as -0.08%)

What it suggests: The perpetual trades at a discount to spot, so short holders pay long holders at settlement.

What it does not tell you yet: It does not prove a bottom is in or ensure a short squeeze.

The easiest mistake to make

Calculating funding cost on initial margin instead of full leveraged notional value and underestimating how 10x leverage magnifies funding drain tenfold; buying blindly just because funding turns negative when downtrends can keep funding negative for weeks; forgetting that you pay or receive funding only if you hold a position at the exact settlement timestamp.

Step 3: read one chart together

This is a made-up reading exercise, not live market data. The point is to move from what you see to a reasonable explanation.

Illustration only: suppose you post $200 in margin at 5x leverage for a long perpetual position on an exchange that settles funding every 8 hours (3 times a day).

  1. Your total position notional value is $200 × 5 = $1,000; suppose the settled funding rate at the 16:00 timestamp is +0.05%.
  2. Your payment for that single interval is Notional $1,000 × 0.05% = $0.50 (five times the $0.10 you would get if you mistakenly multiplied $200 margin by 0.05%).
  3. If all 3 daily settlements stay at +0.05%, one day's funding totals $0.50 × 3 = $1.50, which equals $1.50 / $200 = 0.75% of your posted margin.

Putting the observations together, you could say

Leverage magnifies funding payments relative to your margin by the exact leverage multiple whenever you hold across a settlement timestamp.

But do not jump to this conclusion: It does not prove price will move in your favor after paying the fee, nor does an estimated rate mid-period lock in the final settled rate.

One last question: did it click?

With 20 minutes left before the 16:00 funding settlement, the estimated funding rate reads +0.08%, and you close your entire perpetual position at 15:50. Will you pay the 16:00 funding fee?

Think first, then open the answer

No. Funding is exchanged only among positions that are open at the exact settlement timestamp (16:00:00); closing at 15:50 means you hold zero position at settlement and pay no funding fee.

A concrete practice sequence

  1. Choose a specific exchange’s perpetual contract. Find its funding-rate data and establish whether the displayed rate is settled or predicted.
  2. Read the settlement interval and sign convention, then practise notional value times rate. Do not combine different exchanges or intervals as though they were the same series.
