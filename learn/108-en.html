Funding Rate

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Perpetual contracts use periodic payments between holders to help keep their price near spot. Commonly, positive funding means longs pay shorts and negative means the reverse; the contract rules govern.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

A periodic tethering fee helps stop two boats drifting too far apart.

A made-up example

Illustration only: a $1,000 notional position and +0.01% funding imply about $0.10 paid by a long for that interval under the stated rules.

Who developed or popularized it?

Modern crypto perpetual mechanism popularized by the BitMEX team; no verified sole originator

BitMEX documents its 2016 perpetual product and funding mechanism. Earlier perpetual-futures ideas and other implementations mean this is not proof of one person's invention of all funding rates.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Find the specific perpetual's funding rate, next payment time and history.
  2. Distinguish estimated next funding from settled funding.
  3. Multiply position notional, not just margin, by the interval rate.
  4. Check each exchange's interval and caps before comparing costs.

The mistake to avoid

Positive funding does not guarantee a fall, nor negative a rise. Leverage magnifies cost relative to margin.

A concrete practice sequence

  1. Choose a specific exchange’s perpetual contract. Find its funding-rate data and establish whether the displayed rate is settled or predicted.
  2. Read the settlement interval and sign convention, then practise notional value times rate. Do not combine different exchanges or intervals as though they were the same series.
