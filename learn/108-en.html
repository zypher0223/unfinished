Funding Rate

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Funding Rate (the periodic payment percentage exchanged directly between long and short holders of a perpetual contract, commonly every 8 hours) asks whether the perpetual contract is trading at a premium or discount to spot and which side pays to hold across the settlement timestamp. It plots around 0% in a lower pane. Beginners often calculate funding on their small margin deposit rather than full leveraged notional value, or assume positive funding ensures an immediate crash.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

To keep the perpetual boat from drifting too far from the spot boat, a tethering fee is exchanged every 8 hours: when the perpetual trades above spot (positive rate), longs pay shorts; when it trades below spot (negative rate), shorts pay longs.

A made-up example

Illustration only: Funding Payment = Position Notional Value × Funding Rate. Suppose you post $200 margin at 5x leverage for a long perpetual position, giving a full notional value of $200 × 5 = $1,000. If the settled funding rate at the 16:00 timestamp is +0.05%, under standard rules you pay $1,000 × 0.05% = $0.50 (five times the $0.10 you would get if you multiplied $200 margin by 0.05%). Across three +0.05% settlements in one day, total funding is $0.50 × 3 = $1.50, or $1.50 / $200 = 0.75% of your margin per day. Leverage magnifies carrying cost, though positive funding does not force an immediate price drop.

Who developed or popularized it?

Modern crypto perpetual mechanism popularized by the BitMEX team; no verified sole originator

BitMEX documents its 2016 perpetual product and funding mechanism. Earlier perpetual-futures ideas and other implementations mean this is not proof of one person's invention of all funding rates.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Check contract specs: locate the perpetual's current funding rate, countdown timer, and settlement interval (8-hour, 4-hour, or 1-hour).
  2. Separate estimated from settled: note that mid-period rates are estimates that fluctuate until the exact settlement timestamp arrives.
  3. Calculate on full notional: multiply your full leveraged position notional value (not just margin) by the rate to see your actual payment or receipt.
  4. Combine with price: compare historical funding extremes in the lower pane against main-chart price structure and Open Interest.
  5. Review and log: inspect strong bull runs in history where funding stayed elevated above +0.05% for weeks while price kept climbing.

The mistake to avoid

Calculating funding cost on initial margin instead of full leveraged notional value and underestimating how 10x leverage magnifies funding drain tenfold; buying blindly just because funding turns negative when downtrends can keep funding negative for weeks; forgetting that you pay or receive funding only if you hold a position at the exact settlement timestamp.

A concrete practice sequence

  1. Choose a specific exchange’s perpetual contract. Find its funding-rate data and establish whether the displayed rate is settled or predicted.
  2. Read the settlement interval and sign convention, then practise notional value times rate. Do not combine different exchanges or intervals as though they were the same series.
