Triangles

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

A triangle is price swinging in a narrowing band between two lines. Ascending and descending triangles are often said to “favour” a direction, but the actual breakout direction is uncertain in advance, and breakouts that fall back inside are common.

DefinitionA triangle forms when lines through the swing highs and through the swing lows converge to the right. Symmetrical: lower highs and higher lows; ascending: roughly flat highs with rising lows; descending: roughly flat lows with falling highs. A breakout is a close beyond either line, and its direction is not known in advance.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like two walls slowly closing in: you will leave by one side eventually, but which side first is uncertain.

A made-up example

Illustration only: highs of 104.0, 103.4 and 102.9 fall while lows of 101.0, 101.5 and 101.9 rise; the lines converge, forming a symmetrical triangle. Only a close above the upper line at that point is an upside breakout.

Who developed or popularized it?

Classical chart pattern; systematised by Richard Schabacker and by Edwards and Magee

Such patterns were described systematically by Richard Schabacker in the 1930s and popularised by Edwards and Magee’s 1948 “Technical Analysis of Stock Trends”; the earliest users are unverified.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Find at least two swing highs and two swing lows and draw a line through each set.
  2. Check that the lines converge and decide whether it is symmetrical, ascending or descending.
  3. Record a breakout only when a bar closes beyond one line, not when a wick pokes out.
  4. If price closes back inside after the breakout, treat it as a failed breakout and watch the other side.

The mistake to avoid

Assuming an ascending triangle must break upward is a common oversimplification; the direction is uncertain and failed breakouts are common.
