Dragon-Tiger List

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

A-share traders often treat the Dragon-Tiger List as a cheat sheet to copy famous speculators or institutions. However, the list discloses aggregated brokerage branches rather than individual accounts, and because it is published after hours, next-day followers frequently become exit liquidity.

DefinitionThe Dragon-Tiger List (Statutory Trading Public Information) is an after-hours regulatory disclosure by Chinese stock exchanges listing the top five buying and selling brokerage branches or trading units for securities hitting abnormal thresholds in daily price deviation, amplitude, turnover, or 3-day cumulative deviation. Daily Price Deviation = Stock Daily Return % - Benchmark Index Daily Return %.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Copy-trading the Dragon-Tiger List is like placing bets based on yesterday's highlight reel; the winning move has already happened.

A made-up example

Illustration only: A main-board A-share closes up 10.00% while its benchmark index rises 2.50%. Its daily price deviation is 10.00% - 2.50% = +7.50%, triggering the +/-7% disclosure threshold. After hours, the list shows a famous brokerage branch bought 50 million RMB. Retail traders chase the stock higher at the next morning's open, right as the branch sells its entire position into their buying demand.

Who developed or popularized it?

Shanghai and Shenzhen Stock Exchange Trading Rules on Abnormal Fluctuations

Under their trading rules, the Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges publish the brokerage branches or trading units with the largest buy and sell amounts in securities that meet abnormal-trading criteria ('public trading information', nicknamed the Dragon-Tiger List). Thresholds have been revised several times and are now tiered by board after the registration-based IPO reform.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Access the 'Trading Public Information' portal on SSE, SZSE, or BSE websites after market close to inspect qualifying tickers.
  2. Verify the specific trigger rule: e.g., main-board daily deviation hitting +/-7%, turnover hitting 20%, amplitude hitting 15%, or 3-day cumulative deviation reaching +/-20%.
  3. Distinguish seat categories: 'Institutional Dedicated' indicates funds or insurers, 'Stock Connect' reflects cross-border flows, and retail branches aggregate thousands of accounts.
  4. Compare the top-5 buying volume against the top-5 selling volume as a share of total daily turnover to gauge concentration.
  5. Avoid chasing stocks next day solely on seat fame; T+1 settlement allows yesterday's top buyers to liquidate immediately into morning retail enthusiasm.

The mistake to avoid

Assuming a brokerage branch name represents a single mastermind trader, or believing 'Institutional Dedicated' seats guarantee long-term holding rather than next-day flipping.
