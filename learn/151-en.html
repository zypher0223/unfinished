Flags & Pennants

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

DefinitionA flag starts with a sharp “pole” (a few large bars), followed by a small parallel channel sloping against the pole (the flag) that retraces no more than half the pole. A pennant has a small converging triangle instead. Both are continuation patterns: a close beyond the flag in the pole’s direction signals continuation.

A flag looks like a small flag on a pole: a sharp rally, then a small drifting pullback, showing the earlier force is only resting. It is a continuation pattern: it waits for a break in the pole’s direction, not a reversal.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like a sprinter jogging a couple of steps to catch breath before running on.

Illustration only: the pole rises from 100 to 104, and the flag drifts between 103.0 and 103.8. Its deepest retracement of 1.0 is less than half the 4.0 pole; only a close above the top of the flag signals continuation.

Who developed or popularized it?

Classical chart pattern; systematised by Richard Schabacker and by Edwards and Magee

Such patterns were described systematically by Richard Schabacker in the 1930s and popularised by Edwards and Magee’s 1948 “Technical Analysis of Stock Trends”; the earliest users are unverified.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Find the pole: several large bars in one direction.
  2. Draw the flag: a small channel against the pole (flag) or a small converging triangle (pennant).
  3. Check that the flag retraces no more than half the pole.
  4. A close above the top of the flag is continuation; a break below its bottom invalidates the pattern.

The mistake to avoid

Calling any pullback a flag without checking that the pole was sharp and the retracement shallow is a common error.
