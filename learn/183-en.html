Beginner Safety: Scams & Money Management

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

The first risk beginners meet is often not a wrong market call but being scammed or betting too much at once. Scripts change, but the pattern repeats: promised high returns, manufactured urgency, and requests for private transfers or account details. Protect your capital first; learning to trade comes after.

DefinitionCommon scams include investment schemes or “copy-trade” offers promising steady high returns, impostors posing as support or friends to obtain codes and recovery phrases, fake websites and apps (phishing), “pig-butchering” schemes where a stranger builds trust over time before steering you into an investment, and pump-and-dump chat groups. The floor for money management: use only money you can afford to lose, cap the risk per trade, and do not trade with borrowed money.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like travelling: guard your passport and wallet before studying the sights; lose the wallet and the best itinerary falls apart.

A made-up example

Illustration only: someone promises “a steady 10% a month.” Compounded, that is 1 × 1.1¹² ≈ 3.14 times in a year and about 9.85 times in two; almost no legitimate investment delivers such steady returns for long, so the promise itself is a red flag.

Who developed or popularized it?

Investor education from regulators; the “Ponzi scheme” is named after Charles Ponzi in the 1920s

Schemes that pay early investors “returns” with later investors’ money are named after Charles Ponzi around 1920. US regulators such as the SEC and FINRA continue to publish alerts about crypto investment fraud, pig-butchering and pump-and-dump schemes.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Treat “capital guaranteed,” “sure profit,” “fixed daily returns” and “limited spots, pay now” as red flags.
  2. Download trading apps only from official sites or app stores, type addresses yourself and avoid unknown links.
  3. Refuse anyone who asks for codes, passwords or recovery phrases, and verify identities through official channels.
  4. Invest only money you can afford to lose and set a maximum loss per trade in advance, for example no more than 1–2% of your capital.
  5. Beware pages asking you to “connect your wallet to claim an airdrop” or “verify your wallet”; they often request approval signatures, so check links through the project’s official channels first (see “Airdrops & Sybil Attacks”).

The mistake to avoid

Sending money because someone posted profit screenshots, or borrowing and adding leverage to “win it back,” turning one loss into a problem that affects your life.
