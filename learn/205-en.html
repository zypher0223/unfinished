DEXs & AMMs

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

DefinitionA decentralized exchange (DEX) lets users trade directly from their own wallets through smart contracts, without handing assets to an exchange. Most DEXs use an automated market maker (AMM): liquidity providers (LPs) deposit two tokens into a pool, and the price follows from the pool’s balances by formula, most commonly the constant product x × y = k. Large trades shift the ratio and cause price impact (slippage); LPs also bear “impermanent loss”.

On a DEX your counterparty is not a person but a pool. You need no account, keep custody of your assets and see new tokens list quickly; the costs are heavy slippage in small pools, plenty of scam tokens, and front-running bots that can place orders around yours (sandwich attacks). Providing liquidity earns fees, but after big price moves the result can trail simply holding.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

A pool is like a balance with apples on one side and oranges on the other: take apples and you must add oranges, and the more it tilts, the dearer the next apple. A bigger pool tilts less for the same trade.

A made-up example

Illustration only: a pool holds 100 ETH and 300,000 USDC, so k = 30 million and the price is about 3,000 USDC/ETH. Buying 10 ETH leaves 90 ETH, so USDC must become 30 million ÷ 90 ≈ 333,333; you pay about 33,333 USDC (before fees), an average of about 3,333, roughly 11% above the starting price. In a pool ten times larger, the same trade moves the price only about 1%.

Who developed or popularized it?

Uniswap V1 (November 2018) put the constant-product AMM into practice; the idea traces to Vitalik Buterin’s 2016–2017 posts

Hayden Adams launched Uniswap V1 in November 2018, V2 with any ERC-20 pair in May 2020 and concentrated-liquidity V3 in May 2021. Curve designed different curves for similar-priced assets such as stablecoins to reduce slippage.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Check pool depth and the estimated price impact before trading; split large trades or use an aggregator.
  2. Set a sensible slippage limit; a very loose one invites sandwich attacks.
  3. Verify the token contract address; same-name fakes are common.
  4. Before providing liquidity, weigh impermanent loss from price moves against the fees you expect.

The mistake to avoid

Treating the DEX price as the “market price”. In a shallow pool one trade can push the price far up or down; it only reflects that pool’s current ratio.
