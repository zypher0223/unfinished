Channel Lines & Overshoots

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

The channel line is the trend’s outer edge. A break above it can be buyers accelerating or a final burst before exhaustion. The close is the key: closing back inside often marks the end of a push, while holding outside and widening is acceleration.

DefinitionIn a rising channel, the channel line is drawn parallel to the trend line on the side of the highs. An overshoot is when a bar’s high goes beyond the channel line. If price then closes back inside, it is an overshoot that fails, common at the end of a push; if closes keep holding above the line and the move speeds up, it is acceleration.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like a car running wide on a bend: either it really accelerates away, or it bounces off the guard rail.

A made-up example

Illustration only: the channel line is about 105.0 at a given bar. The bar reaches 105.6 but closes at 104.7, so its high overshoots the line but its close is back inside: an overshoot that failed.

Who developed or popularized it?

Taught systematically by Al Brooks and other price-action educators; no single inventor verified

Channels and parallel lines are classical tools; channel-line overshoots are discussed often in price-action teaching such as Al Brooks’s.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Draw the trend line through two or more lows and a parallel channel line through a high.
  2. Watch for bars whose high goes beyond the channel line.
  3. Check the close: back inside is a failed overshoot; repeated closes outside are acceleration.
  4. After a failed overshoot, only a close below the trend line suggests the channel may be ending; otherwise the channel is intact.

The mistake to avoid

Chasing every break above the channel line as acceleration is a common error; many overshoots are back inside by the close.
