Volume Footprint

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Volume Footprint (a chart type that opens up each candle into horizontal price rows and displays classified sell volume and buy volume side by side at every price step) asks how much volume traded at each specific price level inside the bar and which side was more aggressive. It replaces standard candles on the main chart. Beginners often assume a large number in a footprint cell means resting institutional orders are still waiting there.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Whereas a standard candle shows only the outside silhouette of a building, a footprint chart removes the facade to list how many sellers and buyers transacted on every single floor; however, it lists completed transactions, not orders still waiting outside.

A made-up example

Illustration only: split a completed candle (Open 100, High 103, Low 100, Close 102) into four 1-point price rows showing (Sell Volume × Buy Volume): Row 100 prints 50 × 150 (total 200), Row 101 prints 100 × 400 (total 500), Row 102 prints 200 × 350 (total 550), and Row 103 prints 300 × 100 (total 400). Total sell volume = 50 + 100 + 200 + 300 = 650, total buy volume = 150 + 400 + 350 + 100 = 1,000, bar volume = 1,650, and net Delta = 1,000 - 650 = +350. Row 102 had peak turnover (550 = bar POC), while at the 103 high sell volume (300) was 3x buy volume (100) before price closed at 102.

Who developed or popularized it?

Footprint chart family

Multiple order-flow vendors provide price-level volume charts. Footprint has also been used as a product name; the generic label does not establish a sole inventor.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

First: why look at this tool?

A Volume Footprint chart opens up each candle to display classified sell volume and buy volume at every price row inside the bar, helping you answer where inside the candle trading was heaviest and which side was more aggressive. It shows completed trades classified by price row, not unfilled resting orders.

When it is most useful

Best used when zooming into key support, resistance, or breakout bars to inspect how volume was distributed across the bar's high-low range. Avoid over-interpreting footprints on illiquid symbols or on platforms that approximate buy/sell splits from 1-minute bars rather than tick data.

Step 1: where exactly do you look?

Find the location first, then the units. Colours differ between platforms, so rely on names and meanings rather than colours.

Expanded candle rows with left/right columns (Bid × Ask volume)
Each row represents a price step inside the candle, commonly showing seller-initiated volume on the left and buyer-initiated volume on the right.
Bar summary footer (Delta and Total Volume) and highlighted POC cell
The footer summarizes net Delta (buy volume minus sell volume) and total bar volume, while an outline marks the price row with the highest volume in that candle.

Step 2: what does a change mean?

You see: Price steps higher with right-side buy volume exceeding left-side sell volume across multiple rows, closing near the high

What it suggests: Classified aggressive buying lifted offers across those price steps and held the advance into the close.

What it does not tell you yet: It does not ensure buying continues on the next bar or identify institutional accounts.

You see: Heavy right-side buy volume prints at the very top row of a bar, yet price drops back to close near the low

What it suggests: Despite aggressive market buying at the high, passive limit sellers absorbed those buys and price failed to hold.

What it does not tell you yet: It is not an automatic short signal without checking broader structure.

You see: Tiny, nearly equal numbers print across a narrow two-tick bar during a quiet session

What it suggests: Activity is thin and balanced with no meaningful directional aggression.

What it does not tell you yet: Small random differences of a few contracts do not carry hidden institutional clues.

The easiest mistake to make

Assuming a print of 500 units in one price cell came from a single whale order when it sums all trades at that price step across the bar (and every aggressive buy matched a passive limit sell); failing to adjust row tick grouping for the asset's price scale; overlooking whether the platform estimates footprints from 1-minute bars.

Step 3: read one chart together

This is a made-up reading exercise, not live market data. The point is to move from what you see to a reasonable explanation.

Illustration only: inspect a completed 5-minute bullish candle with Open 100, High 103, Low 100, and Close 102, grouped into four 1-point price rows.

  1. Row volumes (Sell × Buy) are: 100 (50 × 150), 101 (100 × 400), 102 (200 × 350), and 103 (300 × 100).
  2. Total sell volume = 50 + 100 + 200 + 300 = 650, total buy volume = 150 + 400 + 350 + 100 = 1,000, total volume = 650 + 1,000 = 1,650, and net Delta = 1,000 - 650 = +350.
  3. Row 102 has the highest combined row volume at 200 + 350 = 550 (bar POC), while at the 103 high sell volume (300) exceeded buy volume (100) before price closed at 102.

Putting the observations together, you could say

Net aggressive volume was +350 overall with peak turnover at 102 (550 units), while sellers pushed back at the 103 peak.

But do not jump to this conclusion: It does not prove that one single whale bought at 101 or that the next bar cannot drop below 100.

One last question: did it click?

If row 105 on a footprint bar shows 'Sell 0 × Buy 500', does that mean there were zero sellers at 105?

Think first, then open the answer

No. Every executed trade requires both a buyer and a seller; 'Sell 0 × Buy 500' means all 500 units were initiated by aggressive buyers lifting resting limit sell orders at 105.

A concrete practice sequence

  1. Find Volume Footprint in the chart-type menu and check plan and symbol coverage.
  2. Zoom into one completed candle. Read the settings for column definitions, row size and colors before comparing both sides at a price. Compare row totals with candle volume instead of guessing trade direction from color.
