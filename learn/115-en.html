Price Action

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Price action studies whether price is stepping up, stepping down or moving sideways, and how strong each push and pullback is. Fewer indicators still requires clear rules.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Watch a walker's steps and retreats before consulting extra instruments.

Who developed or popularized it?

通用方法；Al Brooks 等为重要当代教师 / Generic method

Reading price movement predates modern courses. Al Brooks is a documented contemporary educator, not a verified sole inventor of price action.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Hide most indicators and mark clear recent highs and lows.
  2. Describe the market in one sentence: rising, falling or ranging.
  3. Compare push and pullback candles by size, overlap and closing location.
  4. Write a paper scenario and failure condition; wait when the situation is unclear.

The mistake to avoid

Trading by feel is not a defined price-action method. A plausible story does not reveal traders' actual motives.

A concrete practice sequence

  1. Keep the candle chart and temporarily hide indicators that obstruct it. Choose one historical period and timeframe.
  2. Mark recent highs, lows and overlapping areas with lines. Advance one completed bar at a time and write what, if anything, changed.
