A beginner’s reading guide · Beginner

Heikin Ashi

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Heikin Ashi averages each bar with the previous one, so colours run in longer streaks and noise drops. The cost is that its prices are not real traded prices, and it reacts more slowly than normal candles.

DefinitionHeikin Ashi recalculates each bar: HA close = (open + high + low + close) ÷ 4; HA open = (prior HA open + prior HA close) ÷ 2; HA high = the maximum of the real high, HA open and HA close; HA low is the minimum. The open and close it displays are not real traded prices.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like drawing a bumpy road as a smooth line of average speed: nicer to look at, but not the real speed at every moment.

A made-up example

Illustration only: a real bar opens at 100, has a high of 101 and a low of 99.6, and closes at 100.8. HA close = (100 + 101 + 99.6 + 100.8) ÷ 4 = 100.35; if the prior HA open was 99.8 and HA close 100.2, the HA open is 100.0.

Who developed or popularized it?

Japanese charting tradition; popularised in the West by Dan Valcu

Heikin Ashi means “average bar” and comes from Japan; it spread in the West after Dan Valcu’s 2004 article in “Technical Analysis of Stocks & Commodities”. The earliest user is unverified.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Switch the chart type to Heikin Ashi.
  2. Watch the length of same-colour streaks and the shadows: bullish bars without lower shadows often mark a strong trend.
  3. Treat colour changes only as hints and switch back to normal candles to check real prices.
  4. Use real prices for orders, stops and backtests, never the prices Heikin Ashi displays.

The mistake to avoid

Using Heikin Ashi closes as real prices for stops or backtests is the most common and most dangerous mistake.
