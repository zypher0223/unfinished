Three White Soldiers & Three Black Crows

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Three white soldiers are three consecutive bullish bars closing near their highs, like soldiers marching forward: buyers stayed in control. But after a run price is stretched, and a pullback often comes first.

DefinitionThree white soldiers: three consecutive bullish bars, each closing above the prior close, each with an upper shadow no more than 30% of its range (closing near the high). Three black crows are the reverse: three bearish bars, each closing lower, each with a lower shadow no more than 30% of its range.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like climbing three steps with firm footing; after hurrying, you still need a breather.

A made-up example

Illustration only: three bullish bars close at 101.0, 101.9 and 102.7, each higher. The third has a high of 102.8 and a low of 101.8, so its 0.1 upper shadow is only 10% of its 1.0 range: it closes near the high, fitting the pattern.

Who developed or popularized it?

Japanese candlestick tradition; popularised in the West by Steve Nison

Candlestick patterns come from Japanese rice-trading tradition; the specific originator is unverified (often attributed to Munehisa Homma without reliable primary sources). Steve Nison’s 1991 “Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques” popularised them in the West.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Find three consecutive bars of the same colour and check that closes rise (or fall) each time.
  2. Check each upper shadow (soldiers) or lower shadow (crows) is no more than 30% of the range.
  3. Consider location: after a decline or a range it matters more.
  4. Watch for overextension: a pullback that holds above the first bar’s open keeps the strength; a break below invalidates.

The mistake to avoid

Chasing three white soldiers after a long rally is a common error; consecutive strong bars are often followed by a pullback.
