Covered Calls & Protective Puts

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

DefinitionA Covered Call combines holding the underlying asset (e.g., 100 shares of stock) with selling 1 corresponding Call option, collecting option premium in exchange for capping upside gains above the strike price—unlike an unhedged naked short call (see the "Options Basics" lesson on this site). A Protective Put combines holding the underlying asset with buying 1 corresponding Put option, paying premium as insurance to put a floor under downside losses.

When an investor already holds spot shares or crypto assets (see the "Spot, Margin & Leveraged Contracts" lesson on this site), options are most commonly used to reshape the risk-reward profile of that existing holding rather than speculate in isolation. A Covered Call trades away upside above a strike in exchange for upfront premium income, while a Protective Put pays an upfront premium to establish a hard floor under downside losses. Knowing exactly what each strategy gives up prevents costly surprises.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

A Covered Call is like a homeowner collecting a non-refundable deposit in exchange for granting a buyer the right to purchase the house at a fixed cap price: you pocket the deposit today, but forfeit any appreciation above the cap. A Protective Put is like buying property value insurance: you pay an out-of-pocket insurance premium so that even if neighborhood prices collapse, your worst-case sale value is floored at the policy strike.

A made-up example

Illustration only: suppose you hold a stock at $100 per share. Case 1 (Covered Call): you sell a $105 strike Call for $2.00 premium, lowering your breakeven price to $100 - $2 = $98; if the stock rallies to $115 at expiration, your shares are called away at $105, capping your maximum profit at ($105 - $100) + $2 = $7 per share (giving up $115 - $105 = $10 of extra upside); if the stock plunges to $80, the $2 premium offers only a small cushion and you still lose ($100 - $80) - $2 = $18 per share. Case 2 (Protective Put): you pay $3.00 to buy a $95 strike Put, raising your breakeven to $100 + $3 = $103; if the stock plunges to $80 at expiration, exercising the $95 Put strictly caps your maximum loss at ($100 - $95) + $3 = $8 per share.

Who developed or popularized it?

Became the two foundational stock-plus-option strategies following the 1973 launch of standardized equity options on Cboe

Following Cboe's introduction of standardized call options in 1973 and put options in 1977, Covered Calls (tracked since 2002 by the Cboe S&P 500 BuyWrite Index, BXM) and Protective Puts (often called Married Puts and linked via put-call parity to synthetic calls) became the foundational stock-overlay strategies.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Before selling a Covered Call, verify that you hold the full underlying quantity (typically 100 shares per standard U.S. equity option contract) so the position does not become a naked short call.
  2. Calculate Covered Call metrics: Maximum Profit = (Strike - Spot Entry) + Premium; Breakeven = Spot Entry - Premium; Downside Risk remains substantial all the way to zero minus the small premium cushion.
  3. Calculate Protective Put metrics: Maximum Loss = (Spot Entry - Strike) + Premium; Breakeven = Spot Entry + Premium; Upside remains uncapped minus the premium paid.
  4. Weigh the explicit trade-offs: a Covered Call sacrifices explosive upside and does not protect against a crash, while a Protective Put protects against a crash at the cost of ongoing insurance drag.

The mistake to avoid

Mistaking a Covered Call for crash protection; the modest premium collected only slightly lowers your cost basis and cannot prevent heavy equity losses if the underlying stock drops sharply.
