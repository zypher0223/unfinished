Volume and Price

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Volume tells you how many people are taking part. If more join as price rises, the move has followers; if a new high comes on less volume, fewer are following. Volume is a supporting clue to read together with location and structure.

DefinitionVolume–price analysis sorts each move into four combinations: rising price on rising volume, rising price on falling volume, falling price on rising volume, and falling price on falling volume. Here, a segment’s average volume above the prior segment’s average counts as rising volume, below as falling. It describes changes in participation and cannot by itself predict direction.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like watching a parade: does the crowd grow as it moves forward, or thin out?

A made-up example

Illustration only: the prior segment averages 12,000 in volume; this rising segment averages 18,000, so it is rising price on rising volume. A later new high averaging only 10,000 is rising price on falling volume.

Who developed or popularized it?

No single inventor verified; covered systematically in the Wyckoff method and elsewhere

Reading volume alongside price goes back to early 20th-century tape reading, and the Wyckoff method treats “effort versus result” systematically; the four combinations are a common teaching scheme.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Split the move into segments: a rise, a pullback or a decline.
  2. Compute each segment’s average volume and compare it with the prior segment.
  3. Place it in one of the four combinations and note what it says about participation.
  4. Judge with location: the same combination means different things inside a range and at a breakout.

The mistake to avoid

Treating “rising volume on a rally is always good, falling volume always bad” as a rule is a common misconception; crypto exchanges also report volume differently.

From the book: cis Stock Trading

Source: cis, cis Stock Trading (Chinese edition, translated by Zhu Jia, China Youth Press, 2020, ISBN 978-7-5153-6031-7; Japanese original published by KADOKAWA, 2018)

What the author says

The author watches which stocks are being bought and sold heavily before reading the news. When strong results still missed expectations and buying thinned out, he sold early; suspicious selling, index inclusion, or steady buying by pensions and funds (‘blind money’) often showed up first in price and volume. These are his experiences; the volume–price rules in this lesson cannot guarantee early detection of insider activity.

This is our English summary of the author’s view; the Chinese page of this lesson carries the original excerpt. The amounts and events are the author’s own experience, mostly in Japanese markets, and nothing here is trading advice.
