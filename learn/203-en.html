Smart Contracts & Token Approvals

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Swapping on a decentralized exchange or depositing in a lending protocol often starts with an “approval”. Many wallets default to an unlimited amount so you need not sign again, but that also means if the contract is later hacked, or the contract you approved was a phishing contract all along, that token in your wallet can be drained in one go.

DefinitionA smart contract is a program on a blockchain that executes automatically when its conditions are met. ERC-20 is Ethereum’s standard interface for fungible tokens (functions such as transfer and balanceOf); ERC-721 is the standard for non-fungible tokens (NFTs). For a contract to move your tokens you must first call approve to grant it an allowance; once granted, the contract can move tokens up to that amount at any time until you revoke it.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

An approval is like giving a cleaning company a spare key: handy for them to come in whenever, but if the company vanishes or the key is stolen, your home is exposed. Taking the key back is revoking the approval.

A made-up example

Illustration only: swapping on a DEX, you grant USDC an unlimited allowance. A year later an old contract of that DEX is exploited, and the attacker calls transferFrom to pull USDC from every address still approving it. Although you have not used it for a year, the 5,000 USDC in your wallet is taken too.

Who developed or popularized it?

Smart contracts were proposed by Nick Szabo in the 1990s; ERC-20 was proposed by Fabian Vogelsteller in 2015 and ERC-721 finalized in 2018

Nick Szabo described “smart contracts” around 1994, using the vending machine as an analogy. Ethereum let arbitrary programs run on-chain; the ERC-20 standard proposed in November 2015 unified token interfaces, and ERC-721, proposed by William Entriken and others, was finalized in 2018 for non-fungible tokens.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Before signing, read the wallet prompt: is it a transfer, an approval, or an off-chain permit signature?
  2. When you can set an amount, approve only what this transaction needs.
  3. Regularly revoke unused approvals with revoke.cash or a block explorer’s approval checker.
  4. Keep long-term holdings in a different wallet from the one you use for daily interactions.

The mistake to avoid

Believing “if I don’t click transfer, I can’t lose money”. Approvals and off-chain signatures can hand over spending rights by themselves, and many phishing thefts happen on a signature users thought was just a login or verification.
