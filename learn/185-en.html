Valuation Multiples: P/E, P/B, and P/S

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Share price alone cannot reveal whether an asset is cheap or expensive. Valuation multiples link share price to profits, equity, or revenue, creating comparative benchmarks across peers while guarding against structural value traps.

DefinitionValuation multiples evaluate relative share pricing against operational fundamentals. The Price-to-Earnings ratio (P/E) = Share Price / Earnings Per Share (EPS); the Price-to-Book ratio (P/B) = Share Price / Book Value Per Share (BVPS); and the Price-to-Sales ratio (P/S) = Share Price / Sales Per Share (SPS). Trailing Twelve Months (TTM) P/E uses historical audited earnings, whereas Forward P/E reflects consensus analyst forecasts.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Multiples are like price per square foot in real estate, standardizing different property sizes into a comparable unit price.

A made-up example

Illustration only: A company's stock trades at $40. Its trailing four quarters cumulative EPS is $2.00, book value per share is $10.00, and sales per share is $8.00. P/E (TTM) = 40 / 2 = 20x; P/B = 40 / 10 = 4x; P/S = 40 / 8 = 5x. If consensus forecasts future EPS at $2.50, Forward P/E is 40 / 2.5 = 16x. Investors currently pay $20 for each dollar of past earnings and $16 against projected forward earnings.

Who developed or popularized it?

Benjamin Graham and David Dodd

Value investing pioneers Benjamin Graham and David Dodd formalized relative equity valuation via earnings multiples and net tangible assets in their 1934 seminal text 'Security Analysis'.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Open your analytical tool to locate P/E (TTM), Forward P/E, P/B, and P/S data for the target ticker.
  2. Verify whether net earnings are positive; for unprofitable high-growth firms with negative EPS, switch to P/S or unit economics.
  3. Compare the company's multiples against direct industry peers and its own 5-year historical percentile bands.
  4. Align valuation with forward revenue growth rates, assessing whether multiple expansion is justified.
  5. Beware cyclical companies showing rock-bottom P/E ratios at peak cycle earnings before profits collapse.

The mistake to avoid

Assuming a low P/E ratio automatically represents a bargain, ignoring that permanent secular decay or debt distress often justifies steep market discounts.
