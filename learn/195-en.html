Limit-up Boards

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Daily price limits in China's A-share market have spawned a unique short-term 'limit-up chasing' culture. Beginners often assume a massive bid wall at the limit price guarantees next-day gains, unaware that resting bids can be canceled instantly, trapping T+1 buyers in steep same-day reversals.

DefinitionA Limit-Up Board occurs when a security hits the maximum daily percentage price increase permitted by the stock exchange; Limit-Up Price = Round(Prior Close * (1 + Limit Percentage), Tick Size). Sealing the board means resting buy orders at Bid 1 lock the price at the upper limit; blowing the board (opening) occurs when sell orders exhaust or buyers cancel the limit-up bid wall, causing prices to retreat; a One-Line Board occurs when Open, High, Low, and Close all equal the limit price.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

A limit-up bid wall is like a crowd of scalpers queued outside a store; it looks massive, but vanishes the second sentiment shifts.

A made-up example

Illustration only: A main-board A-share (10% limit) closes at 100.00 RMB. It hits limit-up on Day 1 at 110.00 RMB (100 * 1.10) and again on Day 2 at 121.00 RMB (110 * 1.10). On Day 3, it touches the 133.10 RMB limit-up price (121 * 1.10), where a trader buys in. Heavy selling then blows the board open, and the stock closes at 119.80 RMB. Locked in by T+1 settlement, the trader suffers an immediate -9.99% unrealized loss on Day 3 alone.

Who developed or popularized it?

SSE and SZSE 1996 Price Limit Regime and 2020-2023 Registration Reforms

The Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges introduced a ±10% daily price limit for stocks and funds on December 16, 1996, with ±5% for ST stocks from 1998; the STAR Market (2019) and ChiNext reform (2020) brought ±20%, and the Beijing Stock Exchange (2021) uses ±30%. From July 6, 2026, main-board risk-warning stocks moved from ±5% to ±10%.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Verify the board's current statutory price limit: +/-10% for SSE/SZSE Main Boards (including ST/*ST risk-warning stocks, raised from +/-5% to +/-10% from July 6, 2026), +/-20% for STAR/ChiNext, and +/-30% for BSE.
  2. Distinguish board structures: low-volume One-Line Boards indicate supply lockup and gap risk, while high-turnover boards reflect heavy chip handovers.
  3. Monitor Bid 1 queue stability; beware of 'spoofing-style' illusions where large bids attract retail queues only to cancel right as large sell orders hit.
  4. Assess liquidity lock-in risk on multi-day consecutive boards (Lianban): when sentiment reverses into a One-Line Limit-Down, sellers cannot exit.
  5. Adjust risk sizing for +/-20% and +/-30% boards, where a failed limit-up reversal can inflict a 25% to 40% loss within a single session.

The mistake to avoid

Treating a large resting Bid 1 order queue as guaranteed price support, forgetting that unfilled limit orders can be pulled in milliseconds right before a dump.
