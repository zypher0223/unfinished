MA Cross

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

MA Cross asks whether the recent short-term average price has moved above or below the longer-term average price. It plots both lines and crossover markers directly on the main price chart. Beginners often treat 'Golden Cross' and 'Death Cross' labels as assured profit signals.

A small worked example, then three interpretations

Separate worked example: these numbers are for hand calculation, not the plotted data.

Already-computed fast values are 99,100,102,104,105; slow values are 101,101,101.5,102,103. The fast average crosses from below to above at point three. Line color does not define the direction.

  1. Above a rising average: recent strength, with pullbacks still possible.
  2. Below a falling average: recent weakness, not a guaranteed decline.
  3. Repeated crossings and a flat average: a range with frequent whipsaws.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

It compares an athlete's scoring average over the last 5 games with their season average: crossing above the season average shows a recent hot streak, but by the time the 5-game average crosses, several big games have already happened.

A made-up example

Illustration only: using a 9-bar short MA and a 21-bar long MA, suppose yesterday the 9-bar MA was 99.0 and the 21-bar MA was 100.0 (gap = 99.0 - 100.0 = -1.0). Today a strong close at 108 lifts the 9-bar MA to 100.8 while the 21-bar MA edges up to 100.3 (gap = 100.8 - 100.3 = +0.5), producing an upward cross. This confirms recent averages have overtaken the longer baseline; it summarizes past gains and does not prevent an immediate pullback.

Who developed or popularized it?

No agreed inventor; Donchian a documented early user

Moving-average crossing has no agreed single inventor. A well-documented early practitioner is Richard Donchian, whose weekly Commodity Trend Timing letter (from 1960 at Hayden Stone) used a 5/20-day crossover; this does not prove he was first.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

First: why look at this indicator?

A Moving Average Cross (MA Cross) plots a shorter-term average price alongside a longer-term average price to compare whether recent prices are stronger or weaker than the broader backdrop. When the shorter average crosses above the longer average, it is called an upward crossover (commonly a 'golden cross'); crossing below is the opposite ('death cross'). A crossover merely records a shift in the relationship between two historical averages—it is not a command that assures profits.

When it is most useful

Helpful when you want to separate 'strengthening over the last few bars' from 'a longer-term backdrop that remains weak.' It is easy to read in sustained trends but prone to whipsaws in sideways ranges. Check whether both lines are already sloping in the same direction before focusing on the crossover point itself.

Step 1: where exactly do you look?

Find the location first, then the units. Colours differ between platforms, so rely on names and meanings rather than colours.

The two moving average lines and their period labels on the main chart
For example, 9 and 21: the 9-period line covers fewer bars and reacts faster, while the 21-period line moves slower. Identify them via the legend rather than assuming red or green is always fast or slow. Both sit on the price axis, where 100 means a price of 100.
The candlestick corresponding to the intersection of the two lines
Look one bar left to see which line was on top, and check the closed bar on the right to see which finished on top—that defines the crossover direction. An intraday cross can uncross before the bar closes, and the intersection level is not your actual execution price.

Step 2: what does a change mean?

You see: The short average crosses above the long average, and both lines subsequently slope upward

What it suggests: The near-term average has improved and is beginning to pull the longer average higher.

What it does not tell you yet: It does not pinpoint the exact start of a rally, nor does it rule out pullbacks after entry.

You see: The short average sits below the long average while both lines slope downward

What it suggests: Both near-term and longer-term averages are weak within an established downward backdrop.

What it does not tell you yet: It does not prove the decline will continue indefinitely.

You see: Both lines are nearly flat and cross back and forth repeatedly

What it suggests: The gap between the two averages is tiny, so minor price noise flips their order.

What it does not tell you yet: Every golden cross or death cross in a flat range is not a brand-new trend.

The easiest mistake to make

Treating 'Golden Cross' or 'Death Cross' names as crystal-ball predictions when both lines are lagging summaries of past bars; mechanically trading every crossover inside a sideways range and suffering repeated whipsaw losses; acting on intraday crosses that uncross before the candle closes.

Step 3: read one chart together

This is a made-up reading exercise, not live market data. The point is to move from what you see to a reasonable explanation.

Hypothetical 9/21 moving averages across three consecutive closed bars: short MA at 99, 100.2, and 101; long MA at 100, 100.1, and 100.4; closing prices at 100, 102, and 103 yuan.

  1. On Bar 1, the short MA (99) sits below the long MA (100), so the recent average is still lower than the longer average.
  2. On Bar 2, the short MA (100.2) finishes above the long MA (100.1), completing an upward crossover.
  3. On Bar 3, both lines rise further, their spread widens from 0.1 to 0.6, and price closes higher at 103, extending the post-cross improvement for another bar.

Putting the observations together, you could say

You can state that the shorter average has turned stronger relative to the longer average and held for an extra bar—while noting that price had already risen before the cross completed.

But do not jump to this conclusion: It does not prove 103 is a bargain entry, nor does it tell whether a pullback will quickly flip the crossover back down.

One last question: did it click?

If the short MA sits above the long MA, but both lines are sloping downward, can you call that a strong uptrend?

Think first, then open the answer

No. Relative position and individual slope are two distinct observations: although the short average is still higher than the long average, both averages are falling together. State both facts rather than letting a 'golden cross' label mask the downward slope.

Once you can say “what I see → what it means → what is still uncertain”, practise with the platform walkthrough below.

TradingView

Search terms: MA Cross

Official built-in using MA9 and MA21 by default; parameters can be changed. Practice setup: Add MA Cross and identify its default 9- and 21-period lines.

  1. Open the TradingView chart using the button below. Choose a stock or coin at the upper left and check the exchange. Choose a chart interval, for example 1 hour or 1 day; one daily candle represents one day.
  2. Use Indicators in the upper chart toolbar, then enter one of the search terms below. Check built-in Technicals first. Community scripts with similar names may calculate different things.
  3. Select the matching result to add it, close the search, and look for a new line or lower pane. If there is no exact built-in match, read the availability note rather than substituting a different indicator.
  4. Hover over the added indicator name and open Settings. Inputs changes calculation settings such as length; start with the defaults. Style changes visual appearance. Follow this lesson’s interpretation steps.
  5. Review completed historical candles and explain what the indicator showed at that time. Adding it to a chart does not place a trade. Practise reading it before any simulated execution.
