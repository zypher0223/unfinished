Basis & Premium

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Basis is the price gap between futures and spot. Futures above spot means the market pays a premium for future long exposure; below spot is the reverse. It is the other side of the funding rate: when a perpetual trades at a large premium, funding is usually positive.

DefinitionThis lesson uses the common crypto convention: basis = futures price − spot price (some texts use spot − futures; check the platform), and premium rate = basis ÷ spot price. Futures above spot is a premium (positive basis, contango); below is a discount (negative basis, backwardation). A dated future’s basis tends toward zero at expiry; a perpetual has no expiry and relies on funding payments to pull it toward spot.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like the price difference between booking next year’s flight and buying today’s: the gap reflects demand for the future and the cost of money.

A made-up example

Illustration only: spot is 60,000 and the quarterly future 61,200, so basis = 1,200 and the premium rate = 1,200 ÷ 60,000 = 2%. As expiry approaches, that 2% usually converges.

Who developed or popularized it?

A standard futures-market concept with no single originator

Basis is a basic concept in commodity and financial futures; crypto perpetual swaps were introduced and popularised by BitMEX in 2016, with funding payments keeping perpetual prices near spot.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Make sure you compare the same underlying at the same moment.
  2. Compute basis and premium rate using the platform’s convention, minding the sign.
  3. For dated futures consider time to expiry (you can annualise); for perpetuals consider the funding rate.
  4. Watch changes, not single readings: a fast-widening or flipping premium shows shifting leverage demand, but cannot alone predict direction.

The mistake to avoid

Reading a positive premium as a “bullish signal” is a common misconception; a high premium can also mean crowded leveraged longs and greater risk of sharp moves.

From the book: cis Stock Trading

Source: cis, cis Stock Trading (Chinese edition, translated by Zhu Jia, China Youth Press, 2020, ISBN 978-7-5153-6031-7; Japanese original published by KADOKAWA, 2018)

What the author says

When bitFlyer’s leveraged bitcoin price traded 26% above spot, the author sold the leveraged contract and bought spot to capture the spread; he adds that a roughly 5% gap between Nikkei futures and the index once existed but has since been closed by arbitrage algorithms. Basis and premium in this lesson are exactly where such spreads come from, and what risks they carry.

This is our English summary of the author’s view; the Chinese page of this lesson carries the original excerpt. The amounts and events are the author’s own experience, mostly in Japanese markets, and nothing here is trading advice.
