Pullbacks & Second Entries

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Pullbacks in a trend often unfold in two legs. The first bounce (H1) is frequently sold, and the second (H2) looks more like the end of the pullback. H1 and H2 only count whether a bar’s high exceeds the prior bar’s high; they are not trade instructions.

DefinitionIn a pullback within an uptrend, the first bar whose high exceeds the prior bar’s high is High 1 (H1). If the pullback then makes a lower low and another bar’s high exceeds the prior bar’s high, that bar is High 2 (H2), the “second entry” signal location. In a downtrend count L1 and L2 the same way using lows below the prior bar’s low.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like pausing twice while walking downstairs: the second pause, looking up, is a better hint that you are turning around.

A made-up example

Illustration only: after a rise to 106, three pullback bars have highs of 105.8, 105.3 and 104.9; the fourth bar’s high of 105.2 exceeds 104.9, so it is H1. If the pullback then makes a lower low and another bar’s high exceeds the prior one, that bar is H2.

Who developed or popularized it?

Al Brooks (systematic bar-counting method)

The H1/H2 and L1/L2 counting method comes from Al Brooks’s price-action books and course; “pullback” itself is a general term.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Confirm the larger direction is up, with rising highs and lows.
  2. Once the pullback starts, compare each bar’s high with the prior bar’s high.
  3. Mark the first bar that exceeds it as H1; if the pullback continues to a lower low, mark the next such bar as H2.
  4. If price later breaks below the low of the pullback that formed H2, the count has failed; start observing again.

The mistake to avoid

A common miscount is calling any bullish bar in the pullback an H1. H1 only asks whether the high exceeds the prior bar’s high, regardless of colour.

From the book: cis Stock Trading

Source: cis, cis Stock Trading (Chinese edition, translated by Zhu Jia, China Youth Press, 2020, ISBN 978-7-5153-6031-7; Japanese original published by KADOKAWA, 2018)

What the author says

The author advises against buying a strong stock on its first sudden dip, seeing it as a fear-driven compromise between chasing and caution; waiting for a pullback that may never come is, in his view, a sign the dip-buying idea is flawed. This lesson’s second entries (H2/L2) instead require a complete pullback and a confirming signal bar; both views reject buying merely because price got cheaper.

This is our English summary of the author’s view; the Chinese page of this lesson carries the original excerpt. The amounts and events are the author’s own experience, mostly in Japanese markets, and nothing here is trading advice.
