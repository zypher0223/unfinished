Stablecoins Basics

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

In crypto, stablecoins often act as “dollars”: for pricing, settlement and stepping aside. But they are tokens issued by a company or protocol, not bank deposits, and their price occasionally drifts from 1 dollar. Know what keeps one pegged before deciding how much to hold and where.

DefinitionA stablecoin is a crypto token designed to hold a target price, most often 1 US dollar. Common types: fiat-reserve (the issuer holds cash, short-term Treasuries and similar reserves, as with USDT and USDC), crypto-overcollateralized (such as DAI), and algorithmic designs that rely on code and incentives. “Stable” is a goal, not a guarantee; trading away from the target is called a depeg.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like a store gift card that says it is worth 100: whether it really is depends on the store still being around and having money behind it.

A made-up example

Illustration only: you buy coins with 1,000 USDC and sell after a drop for 950 USDC; the loss is about 50 dollars because USDC stayed near 1 dollar throughout. If a stablecoin briefly trades at 0.97, 1,000 of them are worth 970 on paper.

Who developed or popularized it?

Tether (USDT) launched in 2014; USDC in 2018

USDT was launched by Tether in 2014, USDC by Circle and partners in 2018, and DAI by MakerDAO. In July 2025 the US signed the GENIUS Act, creating a federal framework for payment stablecoins; rules elsewhere are still evolving.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Find out the type and issuer: reserve-backed, overcollateralized or algorithmic.
  2. Learn which chains it is issued on and whether your exchange redeems or trades it smoothly.
  3. Check whether its recent price has stayed close to 1 dollar and note any history of depegs.
  4. Avoid concentrating everything in one stablecoin; for reserve and attestation details, see this site’s information lesson on stablecoins.
  5. Overcollateralized stablecoins such as DAI rely on lending liquidations to stay stable and can come under stress when collateral crashes or liquidations jam; see “DeFi Lending & Liquidation”.

The mistake to avoid

Treating stablecoins as risk-free cash: the algorithmic UST depegged and collapsed in 2022, and in March 2023 USDC briefly fell below 1 dollar when a bank holding part of its reserves failed.
