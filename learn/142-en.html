Shooting Star & Inverted Hammer

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

The shooting star and inverted hammer are another same-shape pair: a small body with a long upper shadow. After a rise, the long upper shadow shows a rally that was pushed back (shooting star); after a decline, it shows buyers testing higher (inverted hammer). Both need the next bar.

DefinitionShape: an upper shadow at least twice the body, a short lower shadow (no longer than the body), and the body in the lower part of the range. After a rise it is a shooting star (bearish context); after a decline it is an inverted hammer (bullish context, needing stronger confirmation).
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like throwing a ball up into the wind: on a hilltop it shows strong wind; in a valley it shows someone trying to throw higher.

A made-up example

Illustration only: open 80.0, close 79.8, high 81.0, low 79.75. The body is 0.2, the 1.0 upper shadow is five times the body and the 0.05 lower shadow is short; after a rise this is a shooting star.

Who developed or popularized it?

Japanese candlestick tradition; popularised in the West by Steve Nison

Candlestick patterns come from Japanese rice-trading tradition; the specific originator is unverified (often attributed to Munehisa Homma without reliable primary sources). Steve Nison’s 1991 “Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques” popularised them in the West.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Measure the body and both shadows and check the ratios.
  2. Look at the prior move: after a rise it is a shooting star; after a decline an inverted hammer.
  3. For a shooting star the high is the invalidation reference; for an inverted hammer it is the confirmation reference.
  4. Wait for the next close: a lower close after a shooting star is follow-through; a close above its high invalidates it.

The mistake to avoid

Treating every long upper shadow in a rally as a top is a common misreading; in strong rallies new highs often follow such bars.
