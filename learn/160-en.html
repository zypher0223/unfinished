Renko Charts

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

DefinitionA Renko chart draws a brick only when price has moved a fixed amount (the brick size): a close at least one brick above the top of the last brick adds an up brick; a reversal needs a close at least two bricks below the top (one brick below the bottom) to add a down brick. The horizontal axis is not time; every brick represents the same price distance.

Renko removes time and shows only how far price has moved. When price chops within a small range no brick is drawn, filtering noise; but moves smaller than a brick are missed, and one brick can represent minutes or days.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like making a note only when you climb a whole step; walking back and forth on the same step does not count.

A made-up example

Illustration only: the brick size is 0.8 and the last brick’s top is 101.6. A close at 102.5 exceeds 101.6 + 0.8 = 102.4, so an up brick is drawn; a close of only 102.2 draws nothing.

Who developed or popularized it?

Japanese charting tradition; popularised in the West by Steve Nison

Renko comes from the Japanese word for brick; Steve Nison introduced it to Western readers in “Beyond Candlesticks” (1994). The earliest user is unverified.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Choose the brick size (fixed or ATR-based) and record it.
  2. Draw a brick only when the close has travelled a full brick.
  3. Consecutive bricks of the same colour show consistent direction; a reversal needs two bricks.
  4. Check time and real prices on normal candles; changing the brick size changes the whole chart.

The mistake to avoid

The most common misunderstandings are ignoring the choice of brick size and thinking the spacing of bricks reflects time; the same data with another brick size can look completely different.
