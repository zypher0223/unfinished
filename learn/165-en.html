Darvas Box

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

The Darvas box is a rule set for framing pauses in a rally: when a new high stalls, box in the pause; when price breaks above the box, draw a higher one. It turns the steps of a trend into executable rules.

DefinitionDarvas box: after price makes a new high, if the next three bars fail to exceed it, that high becomes the box top; once the top is set, if a subsequent low is not broken by the next three bars, that low becomes the box bottom. A close above the top is an upside breakout that starts a new box; a break below the bottom invalidates the box.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like the landings of a staircase: rest on one, then climb to the next.

A made-up example

Illustration only: price makes a new high at 105.0, and the next three highs of 104.8, 104.6 and 104.9 fail to exceed it, so the top is 105.0. A later low of 103.6 is not broken by the next three bars, so the bottom is 103.6. A close above 105.0 is a breakout.

Who developed or popularized it?

Nicolas Darvas

Dancer Nicolas Darvas described the method in “How I Made $2,000,000 in the Stock Market” (1960); the profit in the title is the author’s own account and is not verified here.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. In a rally, find a new high and check that the next three bars fail to exceed it: that is the top.
  2. Once the top is set, find a low and check that the next three bars do not break it: that is the bottom.
  3. A close above the top is a breakout; draw the next box by the same rules.
  4. A close below the bottom invalidates the box.

The mistake to avoid

Drawing boxes freehand without the rules, or “anticipating” a breakout before the top is confirmed, strips the method of meaning.
