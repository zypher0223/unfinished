Divergence

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Divergence is price and the indicator “disagreeing”: price makes a new high but the indicator does not. It warns that momentum is fading, but it is not a sell instruction; divergence can repeat several times while price keeps rising.

DefinitionRegular bearish divergence: price’s second swing high is above the first, while the indicator’s value at that same bar is below its first high; regular bullish divergence is the reverse. Hidden divergence: price makes a lower high (or higher low) while the indicator makes a higher high (or lower low), often read as a sign of trend continuation. Price swings and indicator values must be aligned on the same bars.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like a speedometer showing the car slowing while it keeps rolling forward; you still have to see whether it stops.

A made-up example

Illustration only: price highs go from 104.0 to 104.5 (higher) while RSI at those bars goes from 72 to 63 (lower): a regular bearish divergence. It only shows weaker momentum; a close below the swing low between the two highs would confirm it.

Who developed or popularized it?

No single inventor verified; spread with oscillators such as RSI and MACD

Divergence between price and momentum was discussed by authors such as Wilder (who published RSI in 1978); the term “hidden divergence” spread later and its originator is unverified.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Find two swing highs (or lows) on price.
  2. Read the indicator at the same bars, not at a nearby indicator peak.
  3. Compare directions: opposite moves are regular divergence; a higher price low with a lower indicator low is hidden divergence.
  4. Wait for price confirmation, such as a close below the swing low between the two highs; until then divergence is only a warning.

The mistake to avoid

The two most common mistakes are matching a price high with a different indicator peak, and treating divergence as an immediate sell signal.
