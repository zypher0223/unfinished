Airdrops & Sybil Attacks

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

DefinitionAn airdrop is a free distribution of tokens to qualifying addresses, typically based on past use of a product (a retroactive airdrop) or on completed tasks and accumulated points. A Sybil attack is one person faking many identities (wallet addresses) to claim shares meant for different users; projects identify and exclude such addresses using funding sources, timing and behavioural similarity on-chain.

In 2020 Uniswap gave each early-user address 400 UNI, starting the era of “use the product, get tokens” and attracting people who farm airdrops for a living. Today’s airdrops lean on points and long tasks, the rules are often revealed afterwards, and participants can lose the time, fees and capital they put in.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

An airdrop is like a mall giving loyal customers vouchers; a Sybil attack is one person queueing a hundred times in a hundred outfits, and once caught, all those vouchers are voided.

A made-up example

Illustration only: a protocol announces a points-based airdrop. You spend USD 300 in fees bridging and trading to earn points, and the tokens you receive are worth USD 150 at launch, half of them locked for six months. Had you used 50 addresses funded from one source, you might also be flagged as a Sybil and lose eligibility entirely.

Who developed or popularized it?

The retroactive airdrop is typified by Uniswap’s UNI in September 2020; the term “Sybil attack” comes from John Douceur’s 2002 paper

On 17 September 2020 Uniswap sent 400 UNI to every address that had used the protocol. Arbitrum’s ARB airdrop in March 2023 used multi-factor points and anti-Sybil filtering. The term “Sybil attack” was coined in 2002 by John Douceur of Microsoft Research, after the book “Sybil” about multiple personalities.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Confirm the announcement comes from the project’s official channels; beware fake sites asking you to connect a wallet to “claim”.
  2. Count the full cost: fees, the opportunity cost of locked funds and the risk to your capital.
  3. Read the unlock schedule; much of what you receive may be locked.
  4. Do not farm with batches of addresses; besides exclusion, some terms explicitly forbid it.

The mistake to avoid

Treating airdrops as risk-free income. Many “claim your airdrop” pages are phishing sites, and one wrong signature can drain a wallet.
