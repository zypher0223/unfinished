Engulfing Patterns

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Engulfing is a two-bar pattern: the second body swallows the whole first body and points the other way. It shows the later side fully reversed the prior bar’s progress, but where it appears and whether it gets follow-through matter more than the shape.

DefinitionBullish engulfing: the prior bar is bearish, the current bar is bullish, and the current body fully covers the prior body (it opens at or below the prior close and closes above the prior open). Bearish engulfing is the reverse. This lesson compares bodies only; texts disagree on whether shadows should count.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like a tug-of-war where the other team gains a step and you pull it back plus a bit more in one go.

A made-up example

Illustration only: the prior bar opens at 101.0 and closes at 100.2 (bearish); the current bar opens at 100.2 and closes at 101.3 (bullish). Its close is above the prior open of 101.0, so its body fully covers the prior body: a bullish engulfing.

Who developed or popularized it?

Japanese candlestick tradition; popularised in the West by Steve Nison

Candlestick patterns come from Japanese rice-trading tradition; the specific originator is unverified (often attributed to Munehisa Homma without reliable primary sources). Steve Nison’s 1991 “Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques” popularised them in the West.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Check location first: a bullish engulfing matters only after a decline.
  2. Check the two bodies: opposite colours, with the second body covering the first.
  3. Note the engulfing bar’s low as the invalidation reference.
  4. Wait for the next close: continuation upward is follow-through; a close below the engulfing bar’s low invalidates it.

The mistake to avoid

The most common misuse is treating an engulfing pattern in the middle of a sideways range as a reversal signal; without a prior trend there is nothing to reverse.
