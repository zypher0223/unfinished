Volume Profile

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Volume Profile (a chart overlay that groups and sums traded volume horizontally by price level over a chosen time window) asks which price zones attracted the heaviest trading turnover and which price zones were passed through quickly. It plots horizontal bars along the side of the main price chart. Beginners often assume the Point of Control (POC)—the longest bar—acts as an unbreakable wall.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Whereas standard bottom volume bars tally sales by day ('Monday vs. Tuesday'), Volume Profile sorts receipts by price tag ('$100 vs. $105') to show where most business took place; however, heavy past business at $105 does not prevent price from repricing to $95 tomorrow.

A made-up example

Illustration only: across a 5-day range from 100 to 105 with total volume of 10M shares split into five 1-point price bins, suppose volume is: 100–101 (1M), 101–102 (2M), 102–103 (4M), 103–104 (2M), and 104–105 (1M). The 102–103 bin has the highest volume at 4M (40% of 10M), placing the POC near 102.5. Adding the two adjacent bins gives 4M + 2M + 2M = 8M shares (80% ≥ the standard 70% Value Area setting), placing VAL at 101 and VAH at 104. This maps past turnover, not a assured floor.

First: why look at this tool?

Volume Profile stacks traded volume horizontally by price level rather than vertically by time, helping you answer which price zones saw the heaviest trading activity over a selected window. Longer horizontal bars show price levels with heavy historical turnover, while short bars show lightly traded price zones.

When it is most useful

Useful after a multi-day range or swing to identify the Point of Control (POC) and surrounding high-volume value area. Avoid relying on a Visible Range profile without locking your zoom, because scrolling the chart changes the bars included and shifts every level.

Step 1: where exactly do you look?

Find the location first, then the units. Colours differ between platforms, so rely on names and meanings rather than colours.

Horizontal volume histogram and Point of Control (POC) line on the main chart
Plotted along the side of the price chart, each row sums volume traded at that price bin; the longest row marks the POC.
Value Area High (VAH) and Value Area Low (VAL) boundaries
The upper and lower borders (commonly shaded brighter) enclosing a set percentage—typically 70%—of total volume in that range.

Step 2: what does a change mean?

You see: Price rotates between VAL and VAH and repeatedly slows near the POC line

What it suggests: Trading remains inside the historical 70% high-volume zone where two-sided activity was previously heaviest.

What it does not tell you yet: It does not make VAL an unbreakable floor or ensure bounces at the POC.

You see: Candles close firmly above VAH into a thin low-volume node

What it suggests: Price has stepped outside the prior high-turnover zone into an area with sparse historical volume.

What it does not tell you yet: It does not prevent sellers from stepping in and pushing price back inside the old value area.

You see: POC and VAH/VAL jump to new prices right after you zoom out on the chart

What it suggests: A Visible Range profile just included older historical bars in its calculation window.

What it does not tell you yet: No new live orders just hit the market; only your chart view changed.

The easiest mistake to make

Treating the POC or Value Area Low as a assured bounce level when POC is often a magnet where price chops sideways; scrolling a Visible Range chart without realizing that changing the visible window recalculates every level; mistaking bar-approximated up/down volume colors for exact tick-level aggressor data.

Step 3: read one chart together

This is a made-up reading exercise, not live market data. The point is to move from what you see to a reasonable explanation.

Illustration only: using Fixed Range Volume Profile across a 5-day range from 100 to 105 with total volume of 10 million shares and a 70% Value Area setting (7 million shares).

  1. By 1-point bins, volume is: 100–101 (1M), 101–102 (2M), 102–103 (4M), 103–104 (2M), and 104–105 (1M).
  2. The 102–103 bin holds the highest volume at 4M shares (4 / 10 = 40%), making ~102.5 the POC.
  3. Combining the POC bin with the two adjacent bins gives 4M + 2M + 2M = 8M shares (exceeding the 7M threshold), placing VAL near 101 and VAH near 104.

Putting the observations together, you could say

You can state that 80% of the 5-day volume occurred between 101 and 104, centered around 102.5.

But do not jump to this conclusion: It does not prove that volume at 102.5 was net buying or that 101 will hold on Day 6.

One last question: did it click?

Last week's VAH is at 104. Today price spikes intraday to 104.3 on one 5-minute bar, then drops back and closes at 103.2. Does this confirm market acceptance above VAH?

Think first, then open the answer

No. A brief intraday poke to 104.3 followed by a close back at 103.2 inside the prior value area shows rejection of the breakout rather than sustained acceptance above 104.
