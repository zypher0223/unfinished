What Crypto Assets Are

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Crypto assets have no company and no dividends, and their prices are set entirely by buyers and sellers, so they often swing far more than stocks. Knowing which chain an asset lives on, how its supply changes and who uses it matters more than watching the price.

DefinitionCrypto assets are digital assets recorded on a blockchain: an ordered chain of transaction records maintained by many nodes, which makes rewriting history costly. A “coin” usually means a blockchain’s native asset, such as bitcoin (BTC) or ether (ETH); a “token” usually lives on an existing blockchain, as many stablecoins do. Circulating market cap = price × circulating supply.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

A blockchain is like a public ledger of which everyone keeps a copy; to rewrite an old page you would have to change most people’s copies at once.

A made-up example

Illustration only: a token priced at 2 with 500 million in circulation has a circulating cap of 2 × 500 million = 1 billion. If all 5 billion tokens were unlocked at the same price, the fully diluted value would be 10 billion, ten times as much, and future unlocks may add selling pressure.

Who developed or popularized it?

Satoshi Nakamoto published the bitcoin white paper in 2008

The bitcoin white paper appeared in 2008 and the network launched in 2009; Ethereum went live in 2015 with smart contracts, and many tokens are issued on Ethereum and similar chains. Satoshi Nakamoto’s real identity remains unverified.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Confirm the asset’s name, ticker and which blockchain it runs on (assets with the same name may exist on several chains).
  2. Check circulating supply, total supply and issuance rules; for example bitcoin is capped at 21 million.
  3. Separate circulating market cap from fully diluted value and watch for future unlocks.
  4. Understand its use and main risks before deciding whether and how much to hold.
  5. To go further into how chains work and how Bitcoin and Ethereum differ, continue with the lessons “Blockchain & the Trilemma” and “Bitcoin vs Ethereum”.

The mistake to avoid

Calling a token cheap by its unit price, for example thinking a 0.001-dollar token has more room to rise than a 1-dollar one; compare market cap and supply instead.
