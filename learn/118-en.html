Wyckoff Method

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Wyckoff combines price and volume to study whether selling pressure is fading and buyers accept higher prices. Common stages include accumulation, markup, distribution and markdown.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Watch an auction: how busy it is, how far bids move, and whether sellers keep offering stock.

A made-up example

Illustration only: repeated tests of a range low have smaller declines and lower volume. Selling may be fading, or activity may simply be quiet; follow-through matters.

Who developed or popularized it?

Richard D. Wyckoff

Wyckoff organized price, volume and trading observations into a method. Later schematics and terminology may extend his original work.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Mark the range boundaries before naming every event.
  2. Compare distance, speed and volume of rallies and declines.
  3. Watch breaks and retests instead of calling every false break a spring.
  4. Treat accumulation or distribution as a hypothesis with contrary evidence and a failure point.

The mistake to avoid

A schematic is not a script the market must follow. Price and volume do not identify an operator's actual plan.
