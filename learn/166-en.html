Turtle Trading

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Turtle trading’s hallmark is writing everything down as rules: when to enter, when to exit, how much to buy. It profits from a few big trends, so it endures many small losses on false breakouts; the hard part is sticking with it.

DefinitionTurtle trading is a rule-based trend-following system. Entry: a break above the highest high of the past 20 bars (System 1) or 55 bars (System 2). Exit: a break below the lowest low of the past 10 bars (System 1) or 20 bars (System 2). Position size uses ATR (called N in the original) so that a one-N move in one unit is worth about 1% of the account; the stop is usually 2N below entry.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like a strict recipe: heat, quantities and timing are fixed, not adjusted by feel.

A made-up example

Illustration only: the 20-bar high is 105.0 and a close at 105.4 breaks it, so enter. If N = 0.8 and the account is 100,000, one unit = 1,000 ÷ 0.8 = 1,250 shares; the stop is 105.4 − 2 × 0.8 = 103.8.

Who developed or popularized it?

Richard Dennis and William Eckhardt (the 1983 Turtle experiment)

In 1983 traders Richard Dennis and William Eckhardt recruited trainees, the “Turtles”, to test whether trading could be taught; the rules were later published by former Turtles such as Curtis Faith. Donchian channels were developed by Richard Donchian.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Use Donchian channels to mark the 20-bar (or 55-bar) high and the 10-bar (or 20-bar) low.
  2. Enter by the rules when price breaks the upper channel, sizing the position with ATR.
  3. Place the stop 2N below entry.
  4. Exit when price breaks the exit channel (10- or 20-bar low); do not try to pick the top.

The mistake to avoid

Copying only the breakout entry while ignoring ATR-based sizing and the exit rules is the most common distortion; the system’s core is sizing and discipline.
