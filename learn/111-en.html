Support and Resistance

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Support is an area where declines previously paused or bounced; resistance is where rises paused or fell back. These are observation areas, not walls.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

People often pause at a doorway, but a crowd can push through it.

Who developed or popularized it?

通用图表分析；单一创始人未确认 / Generic chart-analysis concept

Support and resistance developed through market practice; a sole inventor is unverified. Authors differ on zones and confirmation rules.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Start on a daily or four-hour chart and find clear repeated turns.
  2. Mark a narrow zone with horizontal lines or a rectangle.
  3. On a return, observe a pause, a break or a completed close beyond it.
  4. Record retests and possible support-to-resistance changes.

The mistake to avoid

Repeated touches do not make a level permanent. Avoid moving levels after every new outcome.

A concrete practice sequence

  1. Choose Horizontal Line or Rectangle in the left drawing toolbar and mark an area with repeated past reactions.
  2. Label the price range in drawing settings. Hide later bars and inspect whether price pauses, rebounds or passes through as each new bar appears.
