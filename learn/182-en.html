Spot, Margin & Leveraged Contracts

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Even with the right direction, spot and high-leverage contracts can end oppositely: if price dips 10% and recovers, a spot holder just rides it out, while a 10× position may be liquidated on the way down. Learn price moves on spot before thinking about leverage.

DefinitionSpot means buying the asset itself with your full funds; a drop is only a paper loss and you are not forced out (unless you borrowed). Leverage uses part of your money as margin to control a larger position, magnifying gains and losses by the position size. Contracts (such as perpetuals and futures) trade price changes without necessarily holding the asset and usually carry leverage; when margin can no longer cover losses, the position is forcibly closed (liquidated). Leverage = position value ÷ margin.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Spot is like buying a house with your own money: a price drop just shrinks it on paper. High leverage is putting down only 10%: a 10% drop wipes out the deposit.

A made-up example

Illustration only: 1,000 of margin opens a 10× long, a position worth 10,000. A 5% rise gains 500, half your margin; a 5% fall loses 500, halving it. Ignoring fees and maintenance margin, a fall of about 10% wipes out the margin, and actual liquidation comes earlier.

Who developed or popularized it?

Margin and futures trading are long-established; perpetual swaps were launched by BitMEX in 2016 and spread across crypto

Futures and margin trading have a long history in traditional markets, and borrowing to buy stocks is also leverage. Crypto perpetual swaps have no expiry and use funding rates to keep prices near spot; launched by BitMEX in 2016, they were adopted by major exchanges.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Know whether you are trading spot or a contract, and read the leverage shown on the order screen.
  2. Compute true leverage as position value ÷ margin, and roughly estimate the adverse move that wipes out margin as 1 ÷ leverage.
  3. Check the liquidation price the platform shows and leave enough room so it does not sit inside ordinary swings.
  4. Contracts also carry holding costs such as funding rates; see this site’s lessons on funding rates and liquidation data.
  5. On-chain loans can be liquidated too: when collateral hits the liquidation price it is sold automatically with a penalty; see “DeFi Lending & Liquidation” for the maths.

The mistake to avoid

Seeing only magnified gains and not equally magnified losses, or believing the right direction cannot lose, then being liquidated before price comes back.
