Chan Theory (Chan Lun)

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Chan analysis organizes candles into turning formations, strokes, segments and overlapping centers using chosen rules. Learn the hierarchy before trying to memorize entry labels.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Letters form words, words form sentences, and sentences form a paragraph; each level has rules.

Who developed or popularized it?

缠中说禅（网络笔名） / Chan Zhong Shuo Chan (pen name)

The documented author used the pen name Chan Zhong Shuo Chan in the Teach You to Trade Stocks essays. A disputed real-world identity is not presented as fact.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Read the author's timeframe and candle-inclusion rules and fix one interpretation.
  2. Process a short candle sample manually and identify top/bottom formations.
  3. Build strokes, then segments and centers using the same rules throughout.
  4. Use replay to distinguish structures confirmed then from unfinished ones.

The mistake to avoid

Software can use different stroke rules. A fully drawn historical structure does not prove a live entry was knowable.

A concrete practice sequence

  1. Record the candle-inclusion, formation and stroke definitions you will use. Draw strokes manually with paths before relying on an automatic script.
  2. Check every stroke against that definition before building segments and centers. Visually separate confirmed and unfinished structures.
