Ultimate Oscillator

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

The Ultimate Oscillator asks how strongly closes sit near the top of their true ranges across three timeframes at once. It plots from 0 to 100 in a lower pane. Beginners are often misled by the word 'Ultimate' or assume 'buying pressure' counts actual order-book buy volume.

Oscillator UOa momentum indicator blending short, medium, and long windows with 4:2:1 weights
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

It is like grading a student by combining weekly, midterm, and term exams with 4:2:1 weights so one lucky quiz does not skew the final score; however, past test averages never ensure the next exam score.

A made-up example

Illustration only: each bar's Buying Pressure BP = Close - min(Low, prior Close), and True Range TR = max(High, prior Close) - min(Low, prior Close). Suppose the BP/TR sum ratios over 7, 14, and 28 bars are 0.70, 0.50, and 0.40. With weights 4, 2, and 1, UO = [(4 × 0.70) + (2 × 0.50) + (1 × 0.40)] / (4 + 2 + 1) × 100 = 4.2 / 7 × 100 = 60. Short-term strength is tempered by longer windows; it uses no volume and ensures nothing.

Who developed or popularized it?

Larry Williams

The original article The Ultimate Oscillator is by Larry Williams, also credited in official documentation.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

First: why look at this indicator?

Blend price-push ratios across short, medium, and long lookback windows (typically 7, 14, and 28 bars) into a single score to reduce the blind spots of any single period length. In the Ultimate Oscillator, 'Buying Pressure' is the name of a price-range formula—not a tally of actual buyer accounts.

When it is most useful

Use it when you want to see whether a near-term rally or pullback is supported across multiple lookback horizons; when checking for divergence, always verify actual price structure rather than trusting the word 'Ultimate' in the title.

Step 1: where exactly do you look?

Find the location first, then the units. Colours differ between platforms, so rely on names and meanings rather than colours.

The Ultimate Oscillator curve in the lower pane
Scaled from 0 to 100 with no currency unit, commonly combining 7-, 14-, and 28-bar windows (weighted 4, 2, and 1), where 30 and 70 serve as general reference levels.
Current close, prior close, and high-low range on the main chart
'Buying Pressure' measures Close minus the True Low (the lower of today's Low and yesterday's Close), divided by True Range; the result blends three windows of price placement and cannot be read as the percentage of buy orders.

Step 2: what does a change mean?

You see: The reading sits above 70

What it suggests: Multi-window closes have consistently finished near the upper end of their true ranges, placing momentum in an elevated zone.

What it does not tell you yet: It does not mean price must drop immediately.

You see: The reading sits below 30

What it suggests: Multi-window closes have consistently landed near their true lows, reflecting weak price placement.

What it does not tell you yet: It is not a low-risk bottom-picking guarantee.

You see: Price prints a higher high while the oscillator peak is lower

What it suggests: The new price high was not matched by equally strong multi-window closing placement, making follow-through worth watching.

What it does not tell you yet: Divergence does not schedule a reversal date and can persist during strong trends.

The easiest mistake to make

Treating the name 'Ultimate' as proof of infallibility; assuming 'Buying Pressure' measures real money flows or volume when the formula uses only price bars; shorting immediately on every reading above 70 or first divergence.

Step 3: read one chart together

This is a made-up reading exercise, not live market data. The point is to move from what you see to a reasonable explanation.

Hypothetical single bar: Prior Close = 100, Low = 98, High = 104, and Close = 103. True Low = min(98, 100) = 98, Buying Pressure = 103 − 98 = 5, and True Range = max(104, 100) − 98 = 6; the full indicator sums these over 7, 14, and 28 bars.

  1. Closing at 103 near the top of the 98–104 true range contributes a strong 5/6 ratio on this bar.
  2. If subsequent bars repeatedly close near their lows, the multi-window weighted average will get pulled down.
  3. Even when the oscillator reaches 75, price on the main chart can continue advancing from 105 to 110 rather than reversing on the spot.

Putting the observations together, you could say

It summarizes where closes land inside true ranges across three horizons—never mistake the formula term 'Buying Pressure' for real capital inflows.

But do not jump to this conclusion: A reading of 75 does not mean buyers accounted for 75% of volume, nor does it imply a 75% chance of a pullback.

One last question: did it click?

If the Ultimate Oscillator drops below 30, does that prove sellers have completely run out of shares to sell?

Think first, then open the answer

No. The formula only looks at past highs, lows, and closes—it has zero visibility into remaining sell orders on the book and cannot guarantee that the decline is over.

Once you can say “what I see → what it means → what is still uncertain”, practise with the platform walkthrough below.

TradingView

Search terms: Ultimate Oscillator

TradingView has official documentation for this indicator; first search built-in technical indicators by the English name. Common windows 7/14/28 on a 0–100 scale; 30/70 are references.

  1. Open the TradingView chart using the button below. Choose a stock or coin at the upper left and check the exchange. Choose a chart interval, for example 1 hour or 1 day; one daily candle represents one day.
  2. Use Indicators in the upper chart toolbar, then enter one of the search terms below. Check built-in Technicals first. Community scripts with similar names may calculate different things.
  3. Select the matching result to add it, close the search, and look for a new line or lower pane. If there is no exact built-in match, read the availability note rather than substituting a different indicator.
  4. Hover over the added indicator name and open Settings. Inputs changes calculation settings such as length; start with the defaults. Style changes visual appearance. Follow this lesson’s interpretation steps.
  5. Review completed historical candles and explain what the indicator showed at that time. Adding it to a chart does not place a trade. Practise reading it before any simulated execution.
