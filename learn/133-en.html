Wedges & Three Pushes

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Price makes new highs again and again, but each one adds less, like a runner tiring. Three pushes only say momentum is fading; only a break of the key low suggests the move may be ending.

DefinitionThree pushes: three consecutive swing highs (or lows) in one direction, each beyond the last, with each advance smaller than the one before. Lines through the highs and the intervening lows converge, forming a wedge in price-action terms. Confirmation requires a close below the swing low that preceded the third push.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like jumping up steps: the first jump is high, the second lower, the third barely makes it.

A made-up example

Illustration only: highs at 103.0, 104.2 and 104.8 add 1.2 and then 0.6, so the pushes are shrinking. The swing low before the third push is 103.6; only a close below 103.6 confirms.

Who developed or popularized it?

Taught systematically by Al Brooks and other price-action educators; no single inventor verified

The wedge is a classical chart pattern; “three pushes” is common in price-action teaching such as Al Brooks’s, stressing three pushes and fading momentum.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Find three swing highs (or lows) in the same direction within a trend.
  2. Measure how far each push extends beyond the previous one and check that the distance shrinks.
  3. Connect the highs and the lows and see whether the two lines converge.
  4. Use the swing low before the third push as the confirmation line: a close below it confirms; a new high that holds invalidates.

The mistake to avoid

Many traders assume a reversal as soon as they count three highs. Three pushes only show weakening momentum; often the trend simply continues after a pullback.
