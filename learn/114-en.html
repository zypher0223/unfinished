Classical Chart Patterns

Chart patterns describe the outline formed by many candles, such as double tops, triangles and flags. Describe the actual swings before discussing possible outcomes.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Who developed or popularized it?

多位历史作者发展；单一创始人未确认 / Developed by multiple chartists

Head-and-shoulders, double tops and triangles are not one person’s single invention. Robert D. Edwards and John Magee’s classic textbook is an important systematic reference, not proof that they first used every pattern.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Learn one pattern first, such as a double top.
  2. Mark the high areas and the middle low with horizontal levels.
  3. Write the confirmation, failure point and timeframe.
  4. Hide future candles in replay and record failed examples too.

The mistake to avoid

People readily see familiar shapes in randomness. Measured targets are conditional estimates, not promises.

A concrete practice sequence

  1. Use the relevant pattern drawing or a simple path to mark completed turning points.
  2. Practise one pattern, draw its boundaries and invalidation first, then observe whether price actually leaves the range. Label it a candidate before confirmation.
