Supply and Demand Zones

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Supply/demand zones mark a small base from which price previously moved away quickly. A strong upward departure creates a candidate demand zone; a downward departure a candidate supply zone.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

A stall sold out quickly at a certain price. On a return, check whether demand still exists.

Who developed or popularized it?

供需经济概念的图表应用；单一创始人未确认

Supply and demand principles long predate modern zone-trading courses. Multiple educators use different zone rules; a sole first creator has not been verified.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Define a base and a fast departure before drawing any zones.
  2. Use a rectangle to record the zone's boundaries and start time.
  3. On a return observe slowing, bouncing or a direct break.
  4. Predefine failure, such as a completed close beyond the far edge, and record failed zones in replay.

The mistake to avoid

Candles cannot prove institutions left orders in an old zone. Too many rectangles make every price look meaningful.

A concrete practice sequence

  1. Use Rectangle on a candle chart to mark the small consolidation preceding a fast departure, recording its bounds and selection time.
  2. Observe the first revisit and record both reactions and failures. A zone is a testable location, not proof that resting orders remain there.
