Gaps

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

DefinitionAn up gap is when a bar’s low is above the prior bar’s high, leaving a price zone with no trading between them; a down gap is the reverse. By location: a breakaway gap occurs as a range boundary breaks, a runaway (measuring) gap in the middle of a trend, and an exhaustion gap near the end of a trend that is soon filled. A gap is filled when later prices trade back into the gap zone.

A gap is an empty space between two bars: one bar ends and the next starts trading higher (or lower). Gaps are common in stocks because the market closes overnight; crypto trades around the clock, so its bars rarely gap.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like a missing stair: your foot lands directly on the next level up.

A made-up example

Illustration only: yesterday’s high was 50.2 and today’s low is 51.0, so nothing traded between 50.2 and 51.0: an up gap. If price later falls back to 50.8, the gap has been partly filled.

Who developed or popularized it?

Systematised in classical charting (Edwards and Magee, among others)

The breakaway, runaway and exhaustion classification appears in classics such as Edwards and Magee’s “Technical Analysis of Stock Trends”; the earliest originator is not verified.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Compare a bar’s low with the prior bar’s high (reverse for a down gap).
  2. Note where the gap appears: at a range breakout, mid-trend, or near the end.
  3. Record the gap’s top and bottom as a zone to watch.
  4. If price soon fills the gap and reverses, treat it as an exhaustion gap; if it stays unfilled and continues, it is a breakaway or runaway gap.

The mistake to avoid

“Gaps always get filled” is a common saying but not a rule: many breakaway gaps stay open for a long time, and crypto bars rarely gap, so stock gap theory does not carry over.
