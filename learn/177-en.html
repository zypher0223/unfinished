Placing Orders: Market vs Limit

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

The most common choice when ordering is “fill it now” or “fill it only at a price I accept.” The first is a market order and the second a limit order. Know what each costs before choosing.

DefinitionA market order fills immediately at the best price available, guaranteeing execution but not price. A limit order sets a price: a buy fills only at that price or lower and a sell only at that price or higher, guaranteeing price but not execution. A limit order may fill partially or sit unfilled; most platforms also let you set how long it lasts, such as day-only or good-till-cancelled (GTC).
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

A market order is telling the stallholder “a kilo at whatever you charge now”; a limit order is saying “I will buy if it drops to 8 a kilo, otherwise not yet.”

A made-up example

Illustration only: the last price is 50.00. You place a buy limit at 49.50; price only dips to 49.60 and rebounds, so nothing fills. A buy limit at 50.20 instead fills at the best available offer, say 50.01, not at 50.20.

Who developed or popularized it?

Basic order types of exchange matching; no single originator

Market and limit orders are the basic order types that exchanges match by price then time priority; extra conditions such as duration vary by exchange and broker. Stop orders and other conditional orders are covered in this site’s “Cutting Losses” lesson.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Decide first whether filling matters more than the price or the reverse.
  2. Use a market order when you need to fill and the instrument trades actively; use a limit order to control price.
  3. With limit orders, set the duration and watch for partial or unfilled orders.
  4. After ordering, check the status in your order history: filled, partially filled, open or cancelled.

The mistake to avoid

Assuming a limit order will fill at that price, or that a buy limit fills at the price you typed; if a lower offer exists, you get the better price.
