Staking, Liquid Staking & Restaking

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Ethereum staking yields have hovered around 3%–4% a year for some time. Liquid staking solves the “locked and unusable” problem, and restaking stacks more yield on top. Each layer adds contract, oracle or slashing risk, and in a panic the staking receipt can trade below the underlying asset (a de-peg).

DefinitionStaking locks tokens in a proof-of-stake network to help validate and earn rewards. Liquid staking (LSD/LST) has a protocol stake for you and gives you a token representing your share (such as Lido’s stETH), which can be traded or used in DeFi. Restaking reuses already-staked assets to secure other services (such as EigenLayer), adding another reward and another layer of slashing risk.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Staking is like a fixed deposit; liquid staking is like getting a transferable certificate of deposit; restaking is pledging that certificate again as security for someone else to earn a second interest stream. The more layers, the more a failure anywhere passes down the chain.

A made-up example

Illustration only: in June 2022, amid market panic, stETH briefly traded around 0.93 ETH. If you had borrowed ETH against 100 stETH and bought more stETH in a leveraged loop, the discount cut your collateral value; once the health factor fell below 1 you were liquidated, even though each stETH still represented staked ETH.

Who developed or popularized it?

Ethereum’s Beacon Chain (December 2020), Lido (December 2020), withdrawals enabled by the Shanghai upgrade (April 2023), EigenLayer restaking (2023)

Ethereum’s Beacon Chain began accepting stakes on 1 December 2020, though staked ETH could not yet be withdrawn; Lido launched that month, issuing stETH so staked positions could circulate. The Shanghai/Capella upgrade enabled withdrawals on 12 April 2023. EigenLayer, founded by Sreeram Kannan, rolled out restaking in stages from 2023.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Know whether you hold the native token, a liquid staking token (LST) or a liquid restaking token (LRT).
  2. Watch the receipt’s ratio to the underlying (such as stETH/ETH); the further it is from 1, the more stressed the market.
  3. Learn the exit routes: how long the redemption queue is and whether you can sell near 1:1 on the market.
  4. Avoid leveraged loops with staking receipts; de-pegs and liquidations compound losses.

The mistake to avoid

Treating staking yield as risk-free interest. It pays for validation and slashing risk, and the more layers you stack, the higher the headline yield and the harder the true risk is to judge.
