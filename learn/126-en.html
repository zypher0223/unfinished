Auction Market Theory

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Auction-market thinking treats price as a continuous search for places where trading is accepted. Repeated activity suggests acceptance; a quick departure suggests rejection.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

A seller quotes too high and gets little business. If a higher price later attracts sustained trade, it may be becoming accepted.

Who developed or popularized it?

J. Peter Steidlmayer、James Dalton 等发展；非单一发明

The modern trading framework is closely linked to Market Profile. Steidlmayer is a key developer and Dalton an important interpreter, but neither claim establishes invention of all auction-market theory.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Choose TPO or Volume Profile and state whether acceptance is measured by time or volume.
  2. Mark the prior session's value area and POC as historical references.
  3. Observe rotation inside the old area versus sustained trading outside it.
  4. Use continued time and activity to assess acceptance, not one breakout candle.

The mistake to avoid

Value here is a statistical trading area, not a company's intrinsic value. Its boundaries can fail.

A concrete practice sequence

  1. Choose either TPO or Session Volume Profile, fix the session and state whether you are measuring time or volume.
  2. Read value-area boundaries and annotate whether price returns quickly after leaving or spends time outside. Keep the two measurement types distinct.
