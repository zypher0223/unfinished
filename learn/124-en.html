Inner Circle Trader (ICT)

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

ICT is a course framework combining session timing, prior highs/lows, structure and areas such as fair value gaps. A common FVG definition is non-overlap between the wicks of the first and third candles in a three-candle sequence.

First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Follow a timed route and wait at checkpoints rather than boarding every time a gap appears.

A made-up example

Illustration only: the first candle's high is 100 and the third candle's low is 103. A common bullish FVG marks 100–103, without guaranteeing a revisit.

Who developed or popularized it?

Michael J. Huddleston（The Inner Circle Trader）

ICT is Huddleston's teaching identity and framework. This does not mean he invented every underlying trend, supply/demand or session concept.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Choose one named ICT model and session rather than mixing the whole curriculum.
  2. Check New York time and daylight saving, then mark prior-day extremes.
  3. Apply the three-completed-candle FVG definition and the model's structure conditions.
  4. Write observation, cancellation and risk rules, then test with replay.

The mistake to avoid

An FVG is not necessarily an exchange area with no trades, and it need not be filled. Daylight saving affects session conversion.

A concrete practice sequence

  1. Match chart time zone and session to the chosen model. Mark the observation window with vertical lines and prior-day extremes with horizontals.
  2. Draw a three-completed-candle FVG with a rectangle, recording prices, time and invalidation. Observe rather than assuming an entry when model conditions are incomplete.
