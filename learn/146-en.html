A beginner’s reading guide · Beginner

Harami

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

A harami looks like a large bar “carrying” a small one: the first body is large and the second is small and sits inside it. It shows the first bar’s force did not continue, but not that direction has reversed.

DefinitionHarami: the second bar’s body lies entirely within the first bar’s body (touching the edge counts), the second body is less than half the first, and the two usually have opposite colours. A harami compares bodies only and ignores shadows; an inside bar (lesson 129) compares the whole high and low.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like a big bag holding a small box: you compare the boxes, not the handles.

A made-up example

Illustration only: the first bar is bullish, opening at 100.0 and closing at 101.5; the second is bearish, opening at 101.5 and closing at 101.1. The second body of 0.4 sits within 100.0–101.5 and is less than half of 1.5: a bearish harami.

Who developed or popularized it?

Japanese candlestick tradition; popularised in the West by Steve Nison

Candlestick patterns come from Japanese rice-trading tradition; the specific originator is unverified (often attributed to Munehisa Homma without reliable primary sources). Steve Nison’s 1991 “Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques” popularised them in the West.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Find a bar with a large body and note its top and bottom.
  2. Check that the next body lies entirely within it and is less than half its size.
  3. Keep it distinct from an inside bar: only bodies are compared, so shadows sticking out do not matter.
  4. Wait for later closes: a close beyond the outer edge of the first body invalidates the harami.

The mistake to avoid

The most common error is confusing a harami with an inside bar: a harami compares bodies, an inside bar compares the whole high and low.
