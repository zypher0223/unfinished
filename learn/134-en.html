Trading Ranges

My learning & reviewBeginner route

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

A trading range is price moving back and forth between two lines, with neither side able to push far. Inside a range, breakouts near the edges often fail and price returns to the middle. The range ends only when one side is broken by a close with follow-through.

DefinitionA trading range is a stretch of bars in which price swings back and forth between roughly fixed upper and lower bounds without a persistent series of higher highs and higher lows (or lower highs and lower lows). Here we identify it by at least two touches of the top and two of the bottom without closes persistently beyond either; it ends when price closes beyond a boundary with follow-through.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like pacing a corridor: the walls at each end stop you until one day a door opens.

A made-up example

Illustration only: price twice rises to about 101.9 and falls back, and twice drops to about 100.2 and bounces, with every close between 100.2 and 101.9; this can be treated as a trading range.

Who developed or popularized it?

Taught systematically by Al Brooks and other price-action educators; no single inventor verified

The range is a classical concept; Al Brooks’s “Trading Price Action Trading Ranges” covers behaviour inside ranges systematically.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Find the upper and lower bounds where price turns, each touched at least twice.
  2. Check that highs and lows are not persistently rising or falling in between.
  3. Near a boundary, watch whether a breakout closes beyond it with follow-through.
  4. Treat a close beyond a boundary with follow-through as the end of the range; otherwise keep treating it as a range.

The mistake to avoid

Beginners often chase moves in the middle of a range, where direction is least clear, while breakouts at the edges often fail.
