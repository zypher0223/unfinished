Bitcoin vs Ethereum

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

Bitcoin and Ethereum are often mentioned together but solve different problems: Bitcoin changes as little as possible and puts security and predictable issuance first; Ethereum keeps upgrading to host all kinds of on-chain applications. That is why their price rhythms, risk sources and news focus differ.

DefinitionBitcoin (BTC) is the first public chain, capped at 21 million coins, with new issuance halving about every 210,000 blocks (roughly four years); it is designed for simple, stable value transfer and storage. Ethereum (ETH) is a programmable blockchain where developers deploy smart contracts; ETH pays network fees (gas), has no fixed supply cap, and since 2021 burns the base fee of every transaction.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Bitcoin is like a vault that is rarely rebuilt, its rules seldom touched; Ethereum is like a city under constant expansion, with more opportunity and more construction risk.

A made-up example

Illustration only: after Bitcoin’s fourth halving in April 2024, the reward per new block fell from 6.25 BTC to 3.125 BTC. On a congested day on Ethereum, many transactions pay high gas and the base-fee portion is burned; if that day’s burn exceeds new issuance, total ETH supply shrinks, otherwise it grows.

Who developed or popularized it?

Bitcoin: Satoshi Nakamoto (2008–2009); Ethereum: Vitalik Buterin and others (2013 white paper, 2015 mainnet)

The Bitcoin network went live in January 2009. Vitalik Buterin released the Ethereum white paper in late 2013; a public crowdsale followed in 2014 and mainnet launched on 30 July 2015. EIP-1559 began burning the base fee in August 2021, and The Merge on 15 September 2022 switched consensus from proof of work to proof of stake.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Separate the uses: BTC is mainly for transfers and storing value; ETH also pays fees for on-chain applications.
  2. Check the supply rules: Bitcoin’s schedule is fixed; Ethereum’s issuance and burn change with network use.
  3. Track the events that matter to each: halvings, miners and ETF flows for Bitcoin; upgrades, gas and layer-2 networks for Ethereum.
  4. Do not apply one coin’s logic to the other; their risks come from different places.

The mistake to avoid

Assuming Ethereum’s lack of a cap means unlimited inflation, or that Bitcoin’s cap means it can only rise. Supply rules are one input; demand matters just as much.
