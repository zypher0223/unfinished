Morning & Evening Stars

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

A morning star tells a small three-bar story: a big drop, a one-bar pause, then a strong bar that recovers more than half of the first bar. The evening star is the reverse.

DefinitionMorning star: the first bar is a long bearish bar; the second is a “star” whose body is less than a third of the first body; the third is bullish and closes above the midpoint of the first body. The evening star is the reverse. Classical definitions require gaps around the star; 24-hour markets rarely gap, so this lesson does not require them.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

Like the darkest moment of the night, a pause, and then the sky starts to brighten.

A made-up example

Illustration only: bar 1 opens at 102 and closes at 100 (body 2); bar 2 opens at 100 and closes at 99.9 (body 0.1, less than a third of 2); bar 3 opens at 99.9 and closes at 101.3, above the first body’s midpoint of 101: a morning star.

Who developed or popularized it?

Japanese candlestick tradition; popularised in the West by Steve Nison

Candlestick patterns come from Japanese rice-trading tradition; the specific originator is unverified (often attributed to Munehisa Homma without reliable primary sources). Steve Nison’s 1991 “Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques” popularised them in the West.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Find a long bar in the trend’s direction and note its body midpoint.
  2. Check that the second bar is a small-bodied star (body under a third of the first).
  3. Check that the third bar reverses and closes beyond the first body’s midpoint.
  4. Use the lowest point of the three bars (for a morning star) as invalidation; a close below it invalidates.

The mistake to avoid

A common loosening is calling any “big down, small, big up” sequence a morning star without checking that the third bar closes past the first body’s midpoint.
