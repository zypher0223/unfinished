Layer 2 & Rollups

My learning & reviewBeginner routeLesson quiz

Records are saved only in this browser. Reading is not the same as mastery.

When Ethereum mainnet is congested a single transaction can cost tens of dollars. A layer 2 is like an express road beside the highway: traffic runs on the express road and regularly files its records with the main road. Users see fees fall from dollars to cents, but also meet new risk points such as withdrawal waits, bridges and sequencers.

DefinitionA layer 2 executes many transactions off the main chain and posts compressed results and proofs back to it (layer 1), cutting fees and raising speed while inheriting as much of the main chain’s security as possible. Rollups are the leading design: optimistic rollups assume transactions are valid and allow a challenge window (commonly about 7 days) for fraud proofs; zero-knowledge (ZK) rollups attach a validity proof to each batch, final once the main chain verifies it.
First chart ever? Read these four ideas first
Price chart and candle
A candle summarizes one chosen time interval. On a daily chart, each candle is one day; it shows the opening, highest, lowest and closing price for that day.
Length or period
A length of 14 usually means 14 candles, not automatically 14 days. On a one-hour chart that means 14 hours of bars.
Main chart and lower pane
The main chart shows price. A lower pane is a separate little chart below it. Its numbers may use completely different units.
Signal
A signal is a condition worth checking, such as one line crossing another. It is an observation, not a guarantee or an automatic order.

Think of it this way

An optimistic rollup is like waving goods through and auditing later, reversing anything reported during the audit window; a ZK rollup is like every shipment carrying an inspection certificate that customs checks before release.

A made-up example

Illustration only: you hold 1 ETH on an optimistic rollup and withdraw through the official bridge to mainnet; you wait about 7 days for the challenge window. A third-party fast bridge delivers in minutes, but costs more and adds that bridge’s own contract risk.

Who developed or popularized it?

Rollups were proposed in the Ethereum community in 2018–2019; Arbitrum and Optimism launched mainnets in 2021

After state channels and Plasma, Ethereum researchers proposed rollups in 2018–2019 and in 2020 adopted a “rollup-centric” scaling roadmap. The Dencun upgrade on 13 March 2024 added EIP-4844 “blob” space, sharply cutting the cost for layer 2s to post data to mainnet.

An attributed developer, popularizer and first user are different claims. A unique first user is stated only when reliable documentation supports it.

Read it one step at a time

  1. Know which layer 2 you are using and whether it is optimistic or ZK.
  2. Learn the official bridge’s withdrawal wait and plan ahead if you need the funds.
  3. Check whether a single operator runs the sequencer; if it goes down, transactions may pause.
  4. Before moving assets in, test with a small amount to confirm the address and network.

The mistake to avoid

Assuming a layer 2 is “as safe as mainnet”. Most are still decentralizing, and upgrade keys, sequencers and bridges can all be single points of failure.
